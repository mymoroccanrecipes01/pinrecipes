Easy Homemade Cucumbers and Onions in Vinegar and Sugar for Fresh Summer Sides

This simple cucumber and onion salad tossed in a tangy vinegar and sugar dressing is a fresh, crunchy side that brightens any meal. The balance of sweet and sour with a hint of dill makes it a perfect summer staple. I usually make it ahead so the flavors meld beautifully.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this cucumber and onion salad recipe many summers ago when my neighbor brought it to a potluck. It was so simple yet incredibly refreshing, a perfect balance of sweet and tangy that made me want to eat it all summer long. Since then, I’ve tweaked it a little to suit my family's tastes, adding just the right amount of sugar and vinegar to keep the cucumbers crisp and flavorful without overpowering the natural freshness. What makes this version stand out is the quick pickling technique — letting the cucumbers and onions sit in the vinegar and sugar mixture for at least 30 minutes allows the flavors to meld beautifully while maintaining a satisfying crunch. I usually make this salad a few hours ahead or even the day before, which really helps the flavors deepen. It’s become a staple side dish in my house, especially for summer barbecues and weeknight dinners when I want something light, bright, and fuss-free. The final result is a vibrant, glossy salad with just the right zing and a touch of sweetness, perfect for pairing with grilled meats or simply enjoying on its own.

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 servings

  • 2 medium cucumbers, thinly sliced
  • 1 medium white onion, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 cup white vinegar
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon fresh dill, chopped (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by washing the cucumbers thoroughly and slicing them as thinly as possible. Thin slices ensure the cucumbers absorb the vinegar and sugar dressing quickly while staying crisp. Use a sharp knife or mandoline for uniform slices. Similarly, peel and thinly slice the onion into rings or half-moons. The thin slicing is key to balancing textures and flavors in the salad. Avoid thick cuts, which can make the salad overly pungent or crunchy in an unpleasant way.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Slices are uniformly thin, translucent, and ready to absorb the dressing.
Step 2
In a medium bowl, whisk together the white vinegar, granulated sugar, salt, and black pepper until the sugar dissolves completely. This step is crucial because undissolved sugar can leave gritty spots in the salad. The vinegar and sugar ratio creates a balanced sweet and sour flavor that complements the freshness of the cucumbers and onions. If you skip dissolving the sugar fully, the dressing won’t coat the vegetables evenly, affecting the overall taste.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: The dressing is clear and smooth with no visible sugar grains.
Step 3
Add the sliced cucumbers and onions to the bowl with the dressing. Toss gently but thoroughly to ensure every slice is coated with the sweet vinegar mixture. This helps the flavors infuse evenly and the vegetables soften slightly while maintaining crunch. Be careful not to over-toss or press down too hard, which can bruise the cucumbers and make the salad watery.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: All slices glisten evenly with dressing and look glossy.
Step 4
Cover the bowl with plastic wrap or transfer the salad to an airtight container. Refrigerate for at least 30 minutes to allow the flavors to meld. For best results, let it sit for 2-3 hours or overnight. The chilling also firms up the cucumbers slightly, giving the salad a refreshing crunch. If you serve it immediately, the flavors will be sharp but less integrated.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes to overnight
✓ Doneness: Salad tastes balanced with softened onions and crisp cucumbers.
Step 5
Before serving, give the salad a gentle stir and taste for seasoning. Adjust salt or pepper if needed. If you like, sprinkle chopped fresh dill on top for a lovely herbal note that brightens the dish. Dill is optional but adds a classic touch that pairs beautifully with the tangy dressing.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salad is evenly seasoned and fragrant with fresh herbs if used.
Step 6
Serve chilled as a side dish to grilled meats, sandwiches, or alongside your favorite summer meals. This salad is best enjoyed cold to highlight its crisp textures and refreshing flavors. It also pairs well with heavier dishes as a palate cleanser.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Salad is cold, crisp, and vibrant on the plate.
Step 7
To keep leftovers fresh, store the salad in an airtight container in the refrigerator. The cucumbers will continue to absorb the dressing, becoming more flavorful but slightly softer over time. Consume within 3 days for the best texture and taste.
⏱ Time: Storage
✓ Doneness: Salad remains glossy and fresh without becoming mushy.
Step 8
If you want to customize this salad, try adding thin slices of radish or a pinch of red pepper flakes for some heat. These additions complement the sweet and sour base while adding complexity. Just add them before refrigerating to let the flavors meld.
⏱ Time: Optional prep
✓ Doneness: Additional ingredients are evenly incorporated and flavored.
Step 9
For a no-sugar version, substitute sugar with a natural sweetener like honey or maple syrup, but use sparingly as these can alter the flavor and texture. Adjust vinegar slightly to maintain the tangy balance. This variation works well if you prefer a less processed option.
⏱ Time: Optional prep
✓ Doneness: Dressing remains balanced and coats vegetables well.

Pro Tips

Slice cucumbers and onions as thinly and evenly as possible using a mandoline.
Thin, uniform slices allow the vinegar and sugar dressing to penetrate quickly and evenly, ensuring every bite is flavorful and crisp. Uneven slices can result in inconsistent texture and taste.
Dissolve sugar completely in vinegar before adding vegetables.
Undissolved sugar can leave gritty spots and uneven sweetness in the salad. Fully dissolving the sugar ensures a smooth, balanced dressing that coats the cucumbers and onions evenly.
Let the salad chill for at least 30 minutes before serving.
Chilling allows the flavors to meld and the cucumbers to firm up, creating a refreshing crunch. Serving immediately results in sharper, less integrated flavors and a less satisfying texture.
Add fresh dill just before serving for best flavor.
Fresh dill loses its aroma quickly when mixed too early. Adding it last preserves its bright, herbal notes and enhances the salad’s overall aroma and taste.
Use a glass or ceramic bowl for marinating.
Metal bowls can react with the vinegar, imparting off-flavors and discoloration. Glass or ceramic is non-reactive and keeps the salad tasting fresh and clean.
Store leftovers in an airtight container in the refrigerator.
This prevents the salad from absorbing other fridge odors and keeps it fresh longer. The flavors will deepen over time, but the crunch may soften after a few days.
Try adding a pinch of red pepper flakes for a subtle heat.
A little spice adds complexity and balances the sweetness and acidity, creating a more dynamic flavor profile without overpowering the salad.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using thickly sliced cucumbers and onions.

Why it happens Many cooks underestimate the importance of thin slicing, thinking chunkier pieces will hold up better.

Fix Use a sharp knife or mandoline to slice as thinly and evenly as possible. Thin slices absorb the dressing faster and stay crisp, avoiding a soggy texture.

Not dissolving sugar fully before combining.

Why it happens Rushing the dressing preparation or stirring insufficiently can leave sugar granules undissolved.

Fix Whisk vinegar and sugar thoroughly until the sugar is completely dissolved and the dressing is clear before adding vegetables.

Skipping the chilling time.

Why it happens Impatience or last-minute preparation leads to serving immediately.

Fix Plan ahead and refrigerate the salad for at least 30 minutes to allow flavors to meld and cucumbers to maintain crunch.

Using metal bowls for marinating.

Why it happens Not knowing metal reacts with vinegar can result in off-flavors.

Fix Always use glass, ceramic, or food-safe plastic bowls to avoid metallic taste and discoloration.

Over-salting the salad.

Why it happens Adding too much salt thinking it will enhance flavor.

Fix Measure salt carefully and adjust after chilling, as the flavors concentrate during marinating.

Variations

Spicy Cucumber and Onion Salad

Add a pinch of red pepper flakes or finely chopped jalapeño to the vinegar and sugar dressing for a gentle heat that contrasts beautifully with the sweet and tangy flavors. This adds a lively kick without overpowering the salad’s crisp freshness.

Note: Adjust the amount of spicy ingredient to your heat tolerance; adding too early can intensify the heat, so add just before serving for milder spice.

Dill-Free Version

Simply omit the fresh dill if you don’t have it on hand or prefer a more straightforward flavor. You can substitute with fresh parsley or chives for a different herbal note that still brightens the salad.

Note: Herbs should be added just before serving to maintain freshness and aroma.

Honey-Sweetened Cucumber Salad

Swap granulated sugar for honey to add a natural floral sweetness. This variation creates a richer flavor profile and works well with grilled meats. Use a mild honey to avoid overpowering the delicate vegetables.

Note: Honey dissolves slower than sugar; warm the vinegar slightly to help dissolve honey fully before mixing.

Radish and Cucumber Mix

Add thinly sliced radishes to the cucumbers and onions for extra crunch and a peppery bite. This variation adds color and texture, making the salad even more visually appealing and flavorful.

Note: Slice radishes thinly to ensure they marinate evenly with the other vegetables.

Low-Sugar Version

Reduce the sugar amount or omit it completely for a more tart salad. This version is great if you prefer a sharper vinegar flavor or are watching sugar intake. Balance with a pinch more salt to enhance flavor.

Note: Expect a more pronounced tang and less mellow sweetness; marinate slightly longer to soften sharpness.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This salad should not be left at room temperature for more than 2 hours as the fresh vegetables and vinegar mixture can spoil quickly. Always cover it tightly if left out briefly.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The cucumbers will soften slightly over time but will remain flavorful. Serve chilled for best texture.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as cucumbers and onions lose their crisp texture and become mushy upon thawing.

Reheating

No reheating needed; serve cold or at room temperature. If desired, bring to room temp for 10-15 minutes before serving to soften flavors slightly.

Make Ahead

Prepare the salad up to a day ahead and refrigerate. This resting time enhances the flavor melding and makes it ready to serve quickly when needed.

FAQ

Can I use other types of vinegar for this recipe?
Yes, you can substitute white vinegar with apple cider vinegar or rice vinegar for a milder, fruitier flavor. However, these vinegars may slightly change the color and taste of the salad. Stick to about 5% acidity vinegar to maintain the right balance.
How long can I store this cucumber and onion salad?
Stored in an airtight container in the refrigerator, this salad keeps well for up to 3 days. After that, the cucumbers may become soggy and lose their crispness. For best texture, consume within the first two days.
Can I make this salad without sugar?
You can reduce or omit sugar if you prefer a more tart salad. Keep in mind that sugar balances the vinegar's acidity and enhances flavor. If skipping sugar, consider adding a pinch more salt or a natural sweetener like honey to maintain balance.
Why are my cucumbers soggy after marinating?
Soggy cucumbers often result from slicing too thickly or marinating for too long. Thin slices absorb the dressing quickly but can become mushy if left overnight. Try slicing thinner and marinating for less time if you prefer more crunch.
Can I add other vegetables to this salad?
Yes! Radishes, bell peppers, or even thinly sliced carrots can be added for extra color and texture. Add them along with the cucumbers and onions so they marinate evenly.
Is it necessary to refrigerate the salad before serving?
Refrigeration is important as it allows the flavors to meld and keeps the salad crisp and fresh. Serving immediately is possible but the flavors won’t be as integrated and the onions may taste sharper.
Can I prepare this salad in advance for a party?
Absolutely! Making this salad a few hours or even a day ahead enhances the flavor. Just keep it refrigerated in an airtight container and stir gently before serving.
What if I don’t have fresh dill?
Fresh dill adds a lovely herbal note but is optional. You can omit it or substitute with fresh parsley or chives for a different but still fresh flavor.
