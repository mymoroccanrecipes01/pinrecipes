Easy Fall Dinner Recipes Healthy: Creamy Pumpkin Spinach Lasagna You’ll Love

This creamy pumpkin spinach lasagna is my go-to for a cozy yet healthy fall dinner. The pumpkin adds a subtle sweetness that pairs beautifully with fresh spinach and creamy ricotta. Perfect for busy weeknights when you want something comforting but nourishing.

Introduction

I first made this creamy pumpkin spinach lasagna a few falls ago when I wanted something cozy but lighter than my usual heavy pasta dishes. What makes this version special is the combination of fresh spinach and pumpkin puree mixed into creamy ricotta, giving it a subtle sweetness and vibrant color without feeling heavy. I love using no-boil noodles to save time, and the secret to keeping it perfectly moist is layering the pumpkin mixture evenly and seasoning it well with nutmeg and sage. The finished dish is comforting with just the right balance of creamy and fresh, making it ideal for chilly autumn weeknights when you want something nourishing but simple. Plus, my family actually asks for it again and again, which is always the best compliment!

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 9 no-boil lasagna noodles
  • 1 cup pumpkin puree (not pumpkin pie filling)
  • 1 1/2 cups ricotta cheese
  • 2 cups fresh spinach, roughly chopped
  • 1 1/2 cups shredded mozzarella cheese
  • 1/2 cup grated Parmesan cheese
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1 teaspoon dried sage
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C). In a medium skillet, heat olive oil over medium heat and sauté the minced garlic until fragrant, about 1 minute. Add the chopped spinach and cook just until wilted, about 2 minutes. This step helps remove excess moisture from the spinach so your lasagna doesn’t get watery. Set aside to cool slightly.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Spinach is wilted but still bright green with no liquid pooling in the pan.
Step 2
In a large bowl, combine pumpkin puree, ricotta cheese, sautéed spinach and garlic, nutmeg, sage, salt, and pepper. Mix gently until evenly combined. This mixture forms the creamy filling that gives the lasagna its signature flavor and moist texture. Be careful not to overmix or the ricotta might become grainy.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth, creamy, and evenly speckled with herbs and spinach.
Step 3
Spread a thin layer of the pumpkin-ricotta mixture on the bottom of a 9x13 inch baking dish. This prevents the noodles from sticking and helps the layers meld together during baking.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Bottom of dish is evenly coated with a thin creamy layer.
Step 4
Arrange 3 no-boil lasagna noodles over the pumpkin layer, slightly overlapping as needed. No-boil noodles absorb moisture from the sauce during baking, so layering evenly is key to avoid dry spots.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Noodles cover the bottom layer without gaps.
Step 5
Spread one-third of the pumpkin-ricotta mixture over the noodles, then sprinkle with a third of the shredded mozzarella. Repeat layering two more times, finishing with mozzarella and Parmesan on top for a golden crust.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Layers are even and the top is fully covered with cheese.
Step 6
Cover the baking dish tightly with foil and bake for 25 minutes. Covering traps steam, allowing noodles to soften and flavors to meld without drying out the top.
⏱ Time: 25
✓ Doneness: Edges of lasagna are bubbling and the foil is slightly steamed underneath.
Step 7
Remove foil and bake uncovered for an additional 5 minutes to brown the cheese on top. This step adds a lovely golden color and slight crispness to the cheese layer.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Top cheese is melted, bubbly, and lightly golden.
Step 8
Let the lasagna rest for 10 minutes before cutting. Resting allows the layers to set and makes serving cleaner with less spreading.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Lasagna is firm enough to slice without falling apart.
Step 9
Serve warm, optionally garnished with fresh sage or a light drizzle of olive oil for extra flavor. Enjoy your cozy and healthy fall dinner!
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Lasagna slices hold shape on the plate and look creamy and inviting.

Pro Tips

Sauté spinach with garlic before adding to the filling.
Sautéing removes excess moisture from spinach, preventing a watery lasagna. It also softens the leaves and infuses them with garlic flavor, making the filling more flavorful and the texture better balanced.
Use no-boil noodles for a quicker, fuss-free lasagna.
No-boil noodles absorb moisture during baking, eliminating the need to pre-cook. This saves time and reduces cleanup, while ensuring perfectly tender layers that meld with the creamy pumpkin filling.
Cover the lasagna with foil during most of the baking time.
Covering traps steam, which softens the noodles and keeps the filling moist. Removing the foil only at the end lets the cheese brown without drying out the dish.
Let the lasagna rest before slicing and serving.
Resting allows the layers to set and firm up, making it easier to cut neat slices. Serving immediately can cause the filling to spill out and the noodles to slide apart.
Season the pumpkin-ricotta mixture well with herbs and spices.
Pumpkin is mild and can taste bland alone. Nutmeg and sage add warmth and depth, while salt and pepper enhance overall flavor. Proper seasoning is key to a delicious dish.
Use whole milk ricotta for the creamiest filling.
Whole milk ricotta has a richer texture and flavor compared to low-fat versions, which can be grainy or dry. The creaminess helps bind the filling and keeps the lasagna luscious.
Drain or blot ricotta if it’s watery before mixing.
Excess liquid in ricotta can make the filling runny and cause the lasagna to be soggy. Using a fine sieve or cheesecloth to drain helps maintain the right consistency.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping sautéing spinach and adding it raw.

Why it happens Some cooks assume fresh spinach can be mixed in directly to save time.

Fix Always sauté spinach with garlic first to remove excess moisture and enhance flavor. This prevents watery lasagna and improves texture.

Using pumpkin pie filling instead of pure pumpkin puree.

Why it happens Pumpkin pie filling is more readily available and looks similar.

Fix Use plain pumpkin puree to avoid added sugar and spices that can alter the flavor. Check the label carefully.

Not seasoning the pumpkin-ricotta mixture enough.

Why it happens Pumpkin’s mild flavor can be misleading, so some assume minimal seasoning is needed.

Fix Add salt, pepper, nutmeg, and sage generously to bring out the best flavor.

Overbaking the lasagna leading to dry edges.

Why it happens Leaving it in the oven too long or at too high a temperature.

Fix Follow baking times closely and cover with foil to retain moisture. Remove foil only for the last few minutes.

Cutting the lasagna immediately after baking.

Why it happens Impatience to serve hot lasagna.

Fix Let it rest 10 minutes to set layers and avoid mess when slicing.

Variations

Add Mushrooms for Earthy Depth

Sauté sliced mushrooms with the garlic and spinach for a deeper, earthier flavor. Mushrooms add umami that complements the pumpkin and cheese beautifully.

Note: Make sure mushrooms release their moisture and brown slightly before mixing to avoid watery filling.

Use Kale Instead of Spinach

Swap spinach for chopped kale for a heartier green with more bite and nutrition. Kale stands up well to baking and adds a lovely texture contrast.

Note: Massage kale with a little olive oil and sauté longer to soften before adding to the filling.

Make It Vegan

Use vegan ricotta and mozzarella alternatives and substitute olive oil for butter. Nutritional yeast adds cheesy flavor. This keeps the dish creamy and satisfying without dairy.

Note: Check that vegan cheeses melt well and adjust seasoning to compensate for milder flavors.

Add Spicy Kick with Red Pepper Flakes

Stir in red pepper flakes to the pumpkin-ricotta mixture for a subtle spicy warmth that contrasts nicely with the sweet pumpkin.

Note: Start with a small amount and taste as you go to avoid overpowering the delicate flavors.

Top with Toasted Pecans

Sprinkle chopped toasted pecans on top after baking for a crunchy texture and nutty flavor that pairs perfectly with pumpkin.

Note: Toast pecans lightly in a dry pan until fragrant but not burnt to enhance their flavor.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

You can keep leftover lasagna covered at room temperature for up to 2 hours safely. Beyond that, it’s best to refrigerate to prevent spoilage.

Refrigerator

Store lasagna in an airtight container for up to 4 days. It may firm up and lose some creaminess but reheating gently restores texture. Serve warm or at room temperature.

Freezer

Freeze lasagna tightly wrapped in foil and plastic wrap for up to 3 months. To thaw, place in the refrigerator overnight. Reheat covered in the oven to retain moisture.

Reheating

Reheat slices in a 350°F oven covered with foil for 15-20 minutes until warmed through. Add a splash of milk or cream if it seems dry. Avoid microwaving as it can make texture rubbery.

Make Ahead

Assemble lasagna a day ahead and keep covered in the refrigerator. Baking the next day allows flavors to meld even more. Alternatively, prepare filling and noodles separately and assemble just before baking.

FAQ

Can I use fresh pumpkin instead of canned puree?
Yes, but fresh pumpkin needs to be peeled, cubed, roasted or boiled until soft, then pureed until smooth. This adds extra time but offers a fresh flavor. Make sure the puree is thick and not watery to avoid soggy lasagna.
Do I have to use no-boil noodles?
No-boil noodles are convenient and soak moisture during baking, but you can use regular lasagna noodles if pre-cooked according to package instructions. Adjust baking time slightly to ensure noodles are tender.
Can I prepare this lasagna gluten-free?
Yes, use gluten-free no-boil lasagna noodles or pre-cooked gluten-free noodles. Ensure all other ingredients like ricotta and spices are gluten-free. Baking times may vary slightly with gluten-free pasta.
How do I prevent the lasagna from being watery?
Sauté spinach to remove moisture, drain ricotta if needed, and avoid adding extra liquid. Cover the lasagna during baking to trap steam but don’t overbake. These steps keep the layers moist but not soggy.
Can I add meat to this recipe?
Absolutely! Browned ground turkey or sausage can be added to the pumpkin-ricotta mixture or layered separately. This adds protein and a savory depth but changes the dish from vegetarian to meat-based.
What’s the best way to reheat leftovers?
Reheat in the oven at 350°F covered with foil to retain moisture, about 15-20 minutes. Add a splash of milk if it seems dry. Avoid microwaving for best texture and flavor.
Can I freeze the lasagna before baking?
Yes, assemble the lasagna in a freezer-safe dish, cover tightly, and freeze. When ready, bake from frozen, adding extra baking time and keeping it covered until heated through.
What can I serve alongside this lasagna?
A simple green salad with vinaigrette or roasted autumn vegetables complements the creamy lasagna well without overpowering it.
