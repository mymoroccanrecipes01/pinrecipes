Easy Fall Cookie Recipes for Kids That Are Perfect for Halloween Baking Fun

These fall cookie recipes for kids are a fun way to celebrate Halloween with buttery, soft cookies shaped like pumpkins and ghosts. I love how the warm spices and creamy buttercream make them extra special. Perfect for baking with the little ones on a crisp autumn afternoon!

Introduction

I first made these fall cookie recipes for kids a few years ago when my nieces came over for a Halloween baking day. We wanted something festive but simple enough for little hands to help with, and these cookies fit the bill perfectly. What makes this recipe stand out is the blend of warm autumn spices combined with a touch of pumpkin puree that keeps the cookies soft and moist — not dry like some sugar cookies can be. Plus, the buttery dough is easy to roll and cut into fun shapes like pumpkins and ghosts, which the kids love decorating with colorful icing. I’ve tried other recipes before that left the cookies too hard or bland, but this one balances flavor and texture just right. The secret is in not overmixing the dough and chilling it before baking, which helps keep the cookies tender. When they come out of the oven, they have a beautiful golden color and just the right amount of chewiness. These cookies are perfect for a cozy afternoon baking session with kids or for a festive treat to share at a Halloween party. I think you’ll find they become a favorite in your family too!

What You'll Need

Approximately 24 cookies

  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 3/4 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/4 cup canned pumpkin puree
  • 1 cup powdered sugar (for icing)
  • 2 tablespoons milk (for icing)
  • Food coloring (orange and white) for decorating

How To Do It

Step 1
In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, baking soda, cinnamon, nutmeg, cloves, and salt. This dry mixture ensures the spices and leavening agents are evenly distributed throughout the dough, which helps the cookies rise properly and have consistent flavor. Avoid skipping this step or mixing unevenly, as it can cause uneven texture and pockets of spice.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are fully combined with no clumps or streaks.
Step 2
In a large bowl, beat the softened butter with granulated sugar until light and fluffy, about 3-4 minutes. This aeration step is key for a tender cookie with a nice crumb. Then add the egg and vanilla extract, beating just until incorporated to avoid overmixing, which can make cookies tough.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Mixture is pale, creamy, and smooth with no visible sugar granules.
Step 3
Mix in the pumpkin puree until just combined. The pumpkin adds moisture and a subtle flavor that keeps the cookies soft. Be careful not to overmix after adding the dry ingredients to maintain a tender texture.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Dough looks cohesive and slightly sticky but not wet.
Step 4
Gradually add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture, stirring gently with a spatula until just combined. Overmixing here can activate gluten and lead to tough cookies, so mix until you see no more flour streaks.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Dough forms a smooth ball, slightly sticky but manageable.
Step 5
Wrap the dough in plastic wrap and chill in the refrigerator for at least 1 hour. Chilling firms up the butter and relaxes gluten, which makes the dough easier to roll and helps the cookies hold their shape during baking.
⏱ Time: 60
✓ Doneness: Dough is firm and cold to the touch.
Step 6
Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Line baking sheets with parchment paper to prevent sticking and ensure even baking.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Oven is fully preheated and ready.
Step 7
On a lightly floured surface, roll out the dough to about 1/4 inch thickness. Use pumpkin and ghost-shaped cookie cutters to cut out shapes, then transfer them to the prepared baking sheets with a thin spatula to prevent distortion.
⏱ Time: 15
✓ Doneness: Cookies are evenly shaped and spaced about 1 inch apart on the sheet.
Step 8
Bake the cookies for 12-15 minutes until the edges are lightly golden but the centers still look soft. Overbaking will dry them out, so watch closely starting at 12 minutes.
⏱ Time: 12
✓ Doneness: Edges are golden and centers appear set but not browned.
Step 9
Remove cookies from the oven and let them cool on the baking sheet for 5 minutes before transferring to a wire rack to cool completely. This prevents breakage and helps them firm up just right for decorating.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Cookies are cool to the touch and hold their shape.
Step 10
To make the icing, whisk powdered sugar with milk until smooth and thick but spreadable. Divide and tint with food coloring as desired. Decorate cooled cookies with piped icing or use a small spoon to spread. Let icing set before serving or storing.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Icing is smooth, glossy, and holds shape after piping.

Pro Tips

Chill the dough for at least an hour before rolling.
Chilling solidifies the butter and relaxes gluten strands, which prevents excessive spreading and tough cookies. It also makes the dough easier to handle and helps the shapes keep their edges sharp during baking.
Use room temperature butter for creaming.
Butter that’s too cold won’t cream well with sugar, and melted butter changes the dough’s texture. Properly softened butter traps air when beaten with sugar, resulting in a lighter, tender cookie crumb.
Don’t overmix after adding flour.
Overmixing activates gluten, making cookies dense and chewy rather than tender. Mix just until the flour disappears for the best texture.
Roll dough between two sheets of parchment paper.
This prevents sticking and reduces the need for extra flour, which can dry out the dough. It also makes transferring cut shapes to the baking sheet easier and keeps cookie edges clean.
Bake cookies until edges are golden but centers still look soft.
Cookies continue to cook slightly as they cool. Removing them at this stage ensures a soft, chewy center rather than a dry, crisp cookie.
Use gel food coloring for icing.
Gel colors are more concentrated and won’t thin the icing like liquid dyes. This keeps the icing thick and perfect for piping detailed decorations.
Let icing set completely before stacking or storing cookies.
This prevents smudging and sticking, preserving the beautiful decorations and the cookies’ texture.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using pumpkin pie filling instead of pure pumpkin puree.

Why it happens Many confuse the two because they come in similar cans. Pie filling contains added sugars and spices, which can throw off the recipe’s balance.

Fix Always use plain canned pumpkin puree for baking. It provides moisture without extra sweetness or flavor that could overpower the cookies.

Overbaking the cookies until fully browned.

Why it happens It’s easy to think cookies should be browned all over to be done, but this dries them out.

Fix Remove cookies when edges are lightly golden and centers still look soft. They’ll firm up as they cool, resulting in tender cookies.

Rolling dough too thin or too thick.

Why it happens Without measuring thickness, cookies can bake unevenly or be too fragile to decorate.

Fix Aim for about 1/4 inch thickness for even baking and sturdy shapes that hold decorations well.

Not chilling dough before rolling.

Why it happens Rushing to bake dough straight away leads to spreading and misshapen cookies.

Fix Chill dough at least 1 hour to firm up butter and relax gluten for better shape and texture.

Using too much flour when rolling out dough.

Why it happens Adding excessive flour to prevent sticking dries out the dough and toughens cookies.

Fix Use minimal flour on the surface or roll between parchment paper to avoid sticking without extra flour.

Variations

Gluten-Free Fall Cookies

Swap the all-purpose flour for a gluten-free baking blend. Add a teaspoon of xanthan gum if your blend doesn’t already contain it to help with structure. Expect a slightly different texture—still soft but a bit more crumbly.

Note: Gluten-free dough may be more delicate; chill well and handle gently when rolling and cutting.

Vegan Pumpkin Cookies

Replace butter with vegan margarine and use a flax egg (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water) instead of a chicken egg. Use plant-based milk in the icing. These cookies will be just as soft with a subtle nutty flavor.

Note: Vegan dough tends to be softer; chilling is especially important for clean shapes.

Spiced Maple Cookies

Add 2 tablespoons of pure maple syrup to the wet ingredients for a richer flavor. Increase cinnamon slightly and add a pinch of ground ginger for extra warmth. These cookies have a deeper autumn taste perfect for maple lovers.

Note: The extra liquid from maple syrup may require slightly more flour; add gradually to achieve the right dough consistency.

Chocolate-Dipped Fall Cookies

After decorating and letting icing set, dip half of each cookie in melted dark or white chocolate. Let chocolate harden on parchment paper for a festive twist that adds a rich flavor contrast.

Note: Cool cookies completely before dipping to prevent melting the icing.

No-Bake Pumpkin Cookie Bites

Combine pumpkin puree, oats, powdered sugar, and spices to form balls, then chill until firm. Roll in cinnamon sugar for a quick, no-bake fall treat that’s kid-friendly and delicious.

Note: No baking required, but chilling time is essential for firmness.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store cookies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. Keep them away from heat or direct sunlight to prevent softening or melting of icing. Cookies maintain their texture best when stored on a single layer with parchment paper between layers.

Refrigerator

Cookies can be stored in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 7 days. Refrigeration may firm up the icing and dough slightly, so let them come to room temperature before serving for best flavor and texture.

Freezer

Freeze cookies in a single layer on a baking sheet first, then transfer to a freezer-safe container or bag for up to 3 months. Wrap tightly to prevent freezer burn. Thaw at room temperature before serving, and avoid thawing in the refrigerator to keep icing intact.

Reheating

These cookies are best enjoyed at room temperature. If you prefer warm cookies, heat gently in a low oven (about 300°F) for 5 minutes, avoiding direct heat to prevent melting icing. Avoid microwaving as it can soften icing and make cookies soggy.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the dough 1-2 days ahead and keep it wrapped in the refrigerator. Roll, cut, and bake on the day you plan to serve. Alternatively, bake cookies and store undecorated for a day, then decorate just before serving to keep icing fresh.

FAQ

Can I substitute fresh pumpkin for canned pumpkin puree?
Fresh pumpkin can be used but must be cooked and pureed until smooth before measuring the same amount as canned pumpkin. Fresh pumpkin has more water content, so you may need to reduce other liquids slightly to maintain dough consistency.
How do I prevent my cookies from spreading too much?
Chilling the dough thoroughly before rolling and baking is key. Also, avoid using melted butter and don't overmix the dough. Baking at the right temperature (350°F) and spacing cookies properly on the sheet helps maintain shape.
Can I make the dough ahead and freeze it?
Yes, you can freeze the dough wrapped tightly in plastic wrap for up to 1 month. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before rolling and cutting. This makes it easy to bake fresh cookies whenever you want.
What if I don’t have all the spices listed?
You can use just cinnamon alone or a pumpkin pie spice blend if you have it. The spices add warmth and flavor but the cookies will still be tasty with cinnamon only.
How do I store decorated cookies without smudging the icing?
Let the icing dry completely, ideally overnight, before stacking. Store cookies in a single layer or separate layers with parchment paper in an airtight container to prevent sticking and smudging.
Can I use regular sugar instead of powdered sugar for icing?
Powdered sugar dissolves smoothly and creates the right texture for icing. Regular granulated sugar won’t dissolve well and will result in a gritty texture, so it’s best to use powdered sugar.
How do I make the cookies more kid-friendly for decorating?
Keep the icing thick enough to pipe easily but soft enough to spread. Use gel food coloring for vibrant colors and provide small piping bags or toothpicks for detailed decorating. Let kids have fun with simple shapes and colors.
Can I make these cookies vegan?
Yes, substitute butter with vegan margarine and replace the egg with a flax egg or applesauce. Use plant-based milk in the icing as well. The texture will be slightly different but still delicious.

Final Thoughts
