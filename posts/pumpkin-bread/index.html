Perfect Fall Pumpkin Bread Recipe with Cinnamon Sugar Swirl for Cozy Mornings

This pumpkin bread is pure autumn comfort with a moist crumb and a sweet cinnamon sugar swirl that makes every bite special. I love baking it for chilly mornings when a warm slice with butter hits just right. It’s perfect for sharing or keeping all to yourself!

Introduction

I first baked pumpkin bread years ago when I wanted a cozy autumn treat that felt like a warm hug in bread form. Over time, I tweaked this recipe to get a moist, tender crumb with just the right spice balance. What makes this version stand out is the cinnamon sugar swirl — it adds a caramelized sweetness that takes every slice to the next level. I also like to add a splash of milk to keep the bread soft and fresh longer. Baking this loaf fills my kitchen with that nostalgic fall aroma, and it’s become a favorite for weekend breakfasts or afternoon snacks. It’s straightforward to make, and even if you’re not an experienced baker, you’ll find it forgiving and rewarding. When you cut into it, the cinnamon sugar swirl oozes slightly and the pumpkin flavor shines through without being overpowering. It’s perfect for sharing with family or enjoying with a hot cup of coffee on a crisp morning. I hope you’ll love this as much as my family does!

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, 8 servings

  • 1 3/4 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground ginger
  • 1 cup canned pumpkin puree
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/4 cup milk
  • For cinnamon sugar swirl: 1/3 cup granulated sugar mixed with 1 tablespoon ground cinnamon

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease a 9x5 inch loaf pan with butter or non-stick spray and line it with parchment paper for easy removal. This step ensures your pumpkin bread won’t stick and will bake evenly. Don’t skip lining the pan if you want a clean slice later. The oven temperature is crucial to get a moist crumb without drying it out.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Pan is fully greased or lined with parchment, oven heated to 350°F
Step 2
In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, baking soda, baking powder, salt, cinnamon, nutmeg, cloves, and ginger. Mixing the dry ingredients well ensures the spices are evenly distributed, so every bite has balanced flavor. If you rush this step, you might get uneven pockets of spice or leavening that affect texture.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are uniform in color and texture with no clumps
Step 3
In a separate bowl, combine the pumpkin puree, granulated sugar, brown sugar, vegetable oil, eggs, vanilla extract, and milk. Whisk until smooth and fully blended. This wet mixture provides moisture and richness, while the sugars add sweetness and help caramelize the crust. The milk adds tenderness to the crumb.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients are smooth, glossy, and homogenous
Step 4
Slowly fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture using a spatula. Mix just until combined — overmixing can develop gluten and make the bread tough. The batter should be thick but pourable with no streaks of flour visible. This gentle folding helps keep the bread tender and soft.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Batter is thick, uniform in color, and slightly lumpy but no dry flour
Step 5
Pour half of the batter into the prepared loaf pan, smoothing it evenly. Sprinkle half of the cinnamon sugar mixture evenly over the batter. Then pour the remaining batter on top and finish with the rest of the cinnamon sugar. This layering creates the signature cinnamon swirl that caramelizes during baking for that irresistible texture and flavor contrast.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Batter layers are visible and cinnamon sugar is evenly sprinkled
Step 6
Using a butter knife or skewer, gently swirl the batter and cinnamon sugar layers together in an S or figure-eight pattern. This step creates the beautiful marbled effect without blending the cinnamon sugar completely into the batter. The swirl should be visible but not overmixed.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Distinct swirls visible on the surface of the batter
Step 7
Bake the pumpkin bread in the preheated oven for 50 to 60 minutes. Start checking at 50 minutes by inserting a toothpick into the center. It should come out mostly clean with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. Baking too long will dry it out, too short and it will be undercooked and dense.
⏱ Time: 50
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted in center comes out with a few moist crumbs but no raw batter
Step 8
Let the bread cool in the pan on a wire rack for 15 minutes before removing it. Cooling slightly allows the bread to set and makes it easier to slice without crumbling. Removing it too soon can cause it to break apart, while waiting too long can make slicing harder.
⏱ Time: 15
✓ Doneness: Loaf feels firm to the touch and releases easily from the pan
Step 9
Once cooled, slice the pumpkin bread with a serrated knife for clean cuts. Serve warm with butter or cream cheese if desired. The crumb should be moist and tender, with the cinnamon sugar swirl caramelized and slightly crispy on top. Enjoy with your favorite hot beverage for a perfect fall treat.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Slices hold together neatly with visible cinnamon swirl

Pro Tips

Use room temperature eggs and pumpkin puree for better batter consistency.
Room temperature ingredients blend more easily, ensuring a uniform batter. Cold ingredients can cause the batter to curdle or mix unevenly, affecting texture and rise.
Don’t overmix the batter once you add the dry ingredients.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, leading to a dense, tough bread rather than a tender crumb. Fold gently just until combined for best results.
Line your loaf pan with parchment paper for easy removal.
This prevents sticking and helps the bread release cleanly, preserving the crust and swirl appearance.
Swirl the cinnamon sugar gently to keep distinct layers.
Too much swirling blends the cinnamon sugar into the batter, losing the signature marbled effect and crispy pockets.
Check doneness with a toothpick starting at 50 minutes.
Ovens vary, so starting to check early prevents overbaking, which dries out the bread and dulls the pumpkin flavor.
Cool the bread in the pan before slicing.
This allows the structure to set, making slicing easier and preventing crumbling.
Store pumpkin bread wrapped tightly to maintain moisture.
Proper storage keeps the bread fresh and soft for several days, preserving the tender crumb and swirl texture.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens Many home cooks think mixing thoroughly is better, but overmixing develops gluten, making the bread tough.

Fix Mix just until dry ingredients disappear. Use a gentle folding motion and stop as soon as the batter looks uniform.

Skipping the cinnamon sugar swirl

Why it happens Some skip this step for simplicity, missing out on the signature flavor and texture contrast.

Fix Prepare and layer the cinnamon sugar as directed. Swirl gently to keep the sweet pockets distinct and caramelized.

Baking at too high a temperature

Why it happens Rushing the bake or using inaccurate oven settings can cause the crust to brown too fast and dry out the bread.

Fix Use an oven thermometer if needed and bake at 350°F for even cooking and moist crumb.

Using pumpkin pie filling instead of puree

Why it happens Pumpkin pie filling contains added sugar and spices that throw off the recipe balance.

Fix Always use plain pumpkin puree for consistent flavor and texture.

Not cooling before slicing

Why it happens Cutting hot bread causes it to crumble and lose shape.

Fix Let the bread cool in the pan for at least 15 minutes before removing and slicing.

Variations

Walnut Pumpkin Bread

Add 1/2 cup chopped walnuts to the batter for a crunchy texture and nutty flavor that complements the spices. Fold them in with the dry ingredients to distribute evenly.

Note: Be careful not to overmix after adding nuts to maintain tender crumb.

Gluten-Free Pumpkin Bread

Substitute the all-purpose flour with a gluten-free baking blend. Add 1/2 teaspoon xanthan gum if your blend doesn’t contain it to improve structure and rise.

Note: Bake a few minutes longer and check doneness carefully as gluten-free flours behave differently.

Maple Cinnamon Pumpkin Bread

Replace half the granulated sugar with pure maple syrup and add an extra 1/2 teaspoon cinnamon for a deeper flavor. Reduce milk slightly to balance liquid.

Note: Maple syrup adds moisture so watch baking time to avoid sogginess.

Vegan Pumpkin Bread

Use flax eggs (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and substitute vegetable oil with melted coconut oil. Use plant-based milk instead of dairy milk.

Note: Flax eggs require a few minutes to thicken before mixing; texture will be slightly denser.

Spiced Pumpkin Chocolate Chip Bread

Fold in 1/2 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips with the dry ingredients for a sweet surprise in every bite. The chocolate pairs perfectly with the warm spices.

Note: Avoid overmixing after adding chips to prevent sinking.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Pumpkin bread can be kept at room temperature for up to 2 days if wrapped tightly in plastic wrap or stored in an airtight container. Avoid leaving it exposed to air to prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 5 days. The bread may firm up when chilled but will regain softness when warmed slightly before serving.

Freezer

Freeze pumpkin bread wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and then foil for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator or at room temperature. Reheat gently in the oven or microwave to restore moisture.

Reheating

Warm slices in a toaster oven at 300°F for 5-7 minutes or microwave for 20-30 seconds with a damp paper towel to keep moist. Avoid overheating to prevent drying.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a day ahead and keep it covered in the refrigerator. Bake fresh the next day for best texture. Alternatively, bake the loaf and freeze slices for quick snacks.

FAQ

Can I use fresh pumpkin instead of canned puree?
Yes, but you'll need to cook and puree the pumpkin first. Choose a sugar pumpkin or pie pumpkin, roast or steam until soft, then puree until smooth. Use the same quantity as canned pumpkin. Fresh pumpkin has more moisture, so you may need to reduce other liquids slightly.
How do I know when the pumpkin bread is fully baked?
Insert a toothpick into the center of the loaf after 50 minutes of baking. It should come out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. If the toothpick has wet batter, bake a few minutes longer and test again.
Can I make this pumpkin bread gluten-free?
Absolutely! Substitute the all-purpose flour with a gluten-free baking blend that contains xanthan gum. You might need to bake a little longer and watch the texture, as gluten-free flours behave differently.
What’s the best way to store leftover pumpkin bread?
Wrap the bread tightly in plastic wrap or store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days, or refrigerate for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze slices wrapped well for up to 3 months.
Can I add nuts or chocolate chips to this recipe?
Yes! Fold in chopped nuts like walnuts or pecans, or semi-sweet chocolate chips with the dry ingredients for extra texture and flavor. Be careful not to overmix after adding them.
Why does my pumpkin bread sometimes turn out dry?
Dry bread often results from overbaking or overmixing the batter. Make sure to bake just until a toothpick comes out with moist crumbs and mix gently to avoid developing gluten, which toughens the crumb.
Can I substitute the vegetable oil with butter?
Yes, melted butter can be used instead of oil for a richer flavor, but it may make the bread slightly denser. Use the same amount and ensure it’s cooled before mixing to avoid cooking the eggs.
Is it necessary to swirl the cinnamon sugar?
The swirl adds a lovely caramelized sweetness and texture contrast that makes this pumpkin bread special. You can skip it if you prefer plain pumpkin bread, but the swirl is highly recommended for flavor and appearance.
