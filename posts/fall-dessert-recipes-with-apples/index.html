This cozy fall dessert recipe features tender cinnamon apple bread that’s bursting with juicy apple chunks and warm spices. The secret is a cinnamon sugar glaze that caramelizes beautifully. Perfect for chilly afternoons or family gatherings!

Introduction

I first made this cinnamon apple bread on a chilly autumn afternoon when I wanted something that felt like fall in every bite. I’d tried a few apple bread recipes before, but they often came out dry or lacked that cozy, spiced warmth I was craving. This version is different because I add sour cream to keep the bread moist and tender, and the cinnamon sugar glaze on top caramelizes beautifully, giving it a perfect glossy finish. The diced apples inside soften during baking but still hold their shape, adding bursts of juicy sweetness that pair wonderfully with the warm cinnamon. I love how this bread smells while it’s baking — it fills the whole house with that comforting aroma that makes you want to curl up with a cup of tea. It’s simple enough for a weekday treat but special enough for weekend gatherings. Plus, it keeps well, so it’s great for making ahead or sharing with neighbors. If you’re after a fall dessert that’s easy, flavorful, and feels like a warm hug, this cinnamon apple bread is it. I hope you enjoy making it as much as my family loves eating it!

What You'll Need

One 9x5-inch loaf, 8 servings

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup sour cream
  • 2 medium apples, peeled, cored, and diced
  • 1/4 cup brown sugar
  • 2 teaspoons ground cinnamon (for glaze)
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter (for glaze)
  • 2 tablespoons honey (for glaze)

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9x5-inch loaf pan. In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, baking soda, salt, and ground cinnamon. This dry mix ensures your bread rises evenly and has that perfect tender crumb. Mixing the spices evenly throughout the flour means every bite has consistent warmth without pockets of overpowering cinnamon.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are combined with no lumps or streaks of spice.
Step 2
In a large bowl, beat the softened butter and granulated sugar until light and fluffy, about 3-4 minutes. This creaming step incorporates air, which helps give the bread a lighter texture. Then add eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition to fully combine and avoid curdling. Stir in vanilla extract for aroma and flavor depth.
⏱ Time: 7
✓ Doneness: Mixture is pale, fluffy, and smooth with no visible egg streaks.
Step 3
Add half of the dry ingredients to the wet, mixing gently to combine but not overmixing—overworking the batter can make the bread tough. Next, fold in the sour cream, which adds moisture and tenderness, then add the remaining dry ingredients. The batter should be thick but smooth. Folding carefully keeps the bread light and airy.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Batter is uniform with no dry flour pockets, thick but pourable.
Step 4
Fold in the diced apples gently, distributing them evenly throughout the batter. The apples add juicy bursts of flavor and texture. Avoid stirring too vigorously to prevent the apples from breaking down too much, which would release excess moisture and make the bread soggy.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Apples are evenly dispersed and batter looks consistent.
Step 5
Pour the batter into the prepared loaf pan and smooth the top with a spatula. This ensures even baking and a neat finish. Tap the pan gently on the counter to release any large air bubbles that might cause holes in the bread.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Batter surface is smooth and evenly spread in the pan.
Step 6
Bake for 50-60 minutes or until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean or with just a few moist crumbs. Start checking at 50 minutes to avoid overbaking, which dries out the bread. The top should be golden brown and slightly caramelized from the cinnamon sugar in the batter.
⏱ Time: 50
✓ Doneness: Bread is golden brown, toothpick mostly clean, and edges pulling slightly away from the pan.
Step 7
While the bread bakes, prepare the cinnamon sugar glaze by melting butter in a small saucepan over low heat. Stir in brown sugar, cinnamon, and honey, cooking until the sugar dissolves and the mixture is smooth and glossy. This glaze adds a sticky, sweet finish that caramelizes on the warm bread.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Glaze is smooth, shiny, and slightly thickened but pourable.
Step 8
Once the bread is out of the oven, immediately brush the warm glaze over the top. The heat helps the glaze soak in slightly and creates a beautiful shiny crust. Let the bread cool in the pan for 15 minutes before transferring to a wire rack to cool completely. This resting time helps the crumb set and prevents it from falling apart when sliced.
⏱ Time: 20
✓ Doneness: Glaze is glossy and slightly hardened; bread is warm but not hot.
Step 9
Slice the bread with a serrated knife, wiping the blade between cuts if needed to keep the slices neat. Serve slightly warm or at room temperature. This bread pairs wonderfully with a pat of butter or a drizzle of cream for an indulgent fall treat.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Slices hold shape with visible apple chunks and moist crumb.

Pro Tips

Use room temperature ingredients for even mixing and better texture.
When ingredients like butter, eggs, and sour cream are at room temperature, they blend together more smoothly and trap air better during creaming. This leads to a lighter, fluffier bread. Cold ingredients can cause the batter to curdle or not rise properly.
Don’t overmix the batter once the flour is added to avoid a tough loaf.
Overmixing develops gluten strands excessively, making the bread chewy or dense instead of tender. Mix just until the dry ingredients disappear for a soft crumb and moist texture.
Gently fold in the diced apples to keep the batter light and prevent sogginess.
Rough mixing breaks down the apples, releasing too much moisture which can make the bread wet and heavy. Folding preserves the apple chunks’ texture and distributes them evenly.
Check the bread early to avoid overbaking and drying out the loaf.
Ovens vary, so begin testing the bread with a toothpick around 50 minutes. Overbaking causes the crumb to dry out and lose tenderness, which is hard to fix once it happens.
Brush the glaze on while the bread is warm to maximize absorption and shine.
Warm bread absorbs the glaze better, creating a sticky, flavorful crust that seals in moisture. If applied cold, the glaze sits on top and doesn’t caramelize as well.
Use a serrated knife to slice the bread cleanly without squashing it.
A serrated knife cuts through the crust and crumb smoothly, preserving the bread’s shape and texture. A dull or straight-edged knife can compress the slices and cause crumbling.
Store leftover bread wrapped tightly to keep it fresh longer.
Exposed bread dries out quickly. Wrapping in plastic wrap or storing in an airtight container maintains moisture and flavor for several days.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using cold butter for creaming

Why it happens Many home cooks skip softening butter due to impatience or oversight, but cold butter doesn’t trap air well.

Fix Plan ahead and leave butter out at room temperature for at least 30 minutes. Properly softened butter should feel soft but not greasy. This ensures a fluffy batter and tender crumb.

Overmixing the batter after adding flour

Why it happens Eager to blend everything, some mix too vigorously, which develops gluten and toughens the bread.

Fix Mix just until no dry flour streaks remain. Use gentle folding motions especially after adding apples to keep the texture light and moist.

Skipping the glaze or applying it cold

Why it happens Some think the bread is sweet enough or forget to add the glaze while warm.

Fix Make the cinnamon sugar glaze and brush it on immediately after baking while the bread is warm. This adds flavor, shine, and moisture sealing.

Using soft or overripe apples

Why it happens Choosing apples that are too soft leads to mushy texture and excess moisture in the bread.

Fix Pick firm, tart apples like Granny Smith or Honeycrisp for the best texture and flavor contrast. Dice evenly and fold gently into the batter.

Baking at too high a temperature

Why it happens Rushing the baking process can cause the bread to brown too fast on the outside while remaining undercooked inside.

Fix Bake at 350°F (175°C) and test with a toothpick starting at 50 minutes. This ensures even cooking and a moist crumb.

Variations

Add chopped walnuts or pecans

For a crunchy texture and nutty flavor, fold in 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or pecans with the apples. This adds a lovely contrast to the soft bread and complements the cinnamon spice.

Note: Add nuts at the same time as the apples and fold gently to avoid breaking the nuts or overworking the batter.

Swap sour cream for Greek yogurt

Use full-fat Greek yogurt instead of sour cream for a tangy twist and similar moist texture. It’s a great substitution if you don’t have sour cream on hand or want a slightly lighter option.

Note: Greek yogurt is thicker, so stir it well before adding to ensure even mixing.

Use brown butter instead of regular butter

Brown the butter before creaming to add a deep, nutty flavor that enhances the cinnamon and apple notes. This gives the bread a more complex, rich taste.

Note: Cool the brown butter slightly before creaming with sugar to avoid melting the sugar.

Add a pinch of nutmeg or allspice

For extra warm spice complexity, add 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg or allspice to the dry ingredients. This subtle change deepens the autumnal flavor profile without overpowering the cinnamon.

Note: Mix spices thoroughly with the flour to distribute evenly.

Make mini muffins instead of a loaf

Divide the batter into a greased mini muffin tin for quick individual servings perfect for on-the-go snacks or lunchboxes. Bake for about 18-22 minutes until golden and a toothpick comes out clean.

Note: Adjust baking time and check early to avoid overbaking small portions.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the bread wrapped tightly in plastic wrap or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days. Keep it away from direct sunlight or heat sources to prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Refrigerate wrapped in plastic or in an airtight container for up to 5 days. The bread may firm up and lose some moisture but will remain safe to eat. For best texture, bring to room temperature before serving or warm gently.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled bread tightly in plastic wrap and then in aluminum foil or a freezer bag. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator or at room temperature. Reheat slices in a toaster oven or microwave to restore freshness.

Reheating

Warm slices in a toaster oven at 325°F for 5-7 minutes or microwave for 20-30 seconds. Adding a small pat of butter or a drizzle of honey enhances moisture and flavor. Avoid overheating to prevent dryness.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter 1 day ahead and keep it covered in the refrigerator. Bring to room temperature before baking. The bread can also be baked and glazed a day ahead; store wrapped tightly and reheat slices before serving.

FAQ

Can I use frozen apples for this recipe?
Frozen apples tend to release excess moisture when thawed, which can make the bread soggy. If you must use frozen, thaw and drain them thoroughly, pat dry with paper towels, and fold gently into the batter to minimize moisture impact.
What if I don’t have sour cream? Can I skip it?
Sour cream adds moisture and tenderness, but you can substitute with full-fat Greek yogurt or buttermilk. Skipping it altogether may result in a drier bread, so it’s best to use one of these alternatives for the best texture.
How do I know when the bread is fully baked?
Insert a toothpick into the center of the loaf. It should come out clean or with just a few moist crumbs attached. The bread should be golden brown and the edges slightly pulling away from the pan.
Can I make this recipe gluten-free?
You can try substituting a 1-to-1 gluten-free baking flour blend, but results may vary. Gluten-free flours can affect texture and rise. Adding a bit of xanthan gum if your blend lacks it can help with structure.
Why is my bread dense or heavy?
Common causes include overmixing the batter after adding flour, using cold ingredients, or not creaming the butter and sugar properly. Follow the steps carefully to incorporate air and avoid toughening the crumb.
Can I add nuts or raisins?
Yes! Adding 1/2 cup chopped nuts or raisins with the apples works well. Fold them in gently to maintain the batter’s texture and add extra flavor and crunch.
How should I store leftover bread?
Wrap leftover bread tightly in plastic wrap or store in an airtight container at room temperature for a couple of days, or refrigerate for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze wrapped tightly for up to 3 months.
Can I double this recipe?
Yes, you can double the ingredients and bake in two loaf pans or a larger pan. Adjust baking time accordingly, starting to check doneness at the usual time and increasing as needed.
