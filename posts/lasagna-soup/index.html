Easy One-Pot Lasagna Soup Recipe for Cozy Weeknight Dinners

This one-pot lasagna soup is the perfect cozy dinner when you want all the flavors of classic lasagna without the fuss. It’s loaded with tender pasta, savory beef, and melty cheese, simmered in a rich tomato broth. Great for chilly nights or busy evenings!

Introduction

I first made this lasagna soup on a chilly evening when I wanted all the cozy comfort of lasagna without the hours of baking. It quickly became a family favorite because it’s hearty, simple, and packed with flavor. What makes this version stand out is cooking the broken lasagna noodles directly in the broth, so they soak up all those rich tomato and beef flavors. Plus, topping it off with melty mozzarella and a sprinkle of Parmesan gives it that irresistible cheesy finish. I’ve tried other lasagna soups before, but this one hits the perfect balance between a stew and a pasta dish — it’s warm, filling, and just a little bit indulgent. It’s also great for busy weeknights since it all cooks in one pot, making cleanup a breeze. When you serve it, you’ll notice the tender pasta ribbons swimming in a savory broth with just the right amount of spice and herb aroma. It’s the kind of meal that feels like a warm hug from the inside out.

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 hearty servings

  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 pound lean ground beef
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1 teaspoon dried basil
  • 1/4 teaspoon red pepper flakes (optional)
  • 4 cups low-sodium beef broth
  • 1 (14-ounce) can crushed tomatoes
  • 8 ounces no-boil lasagna noodles, broken into pieces
  • 1/2 cup grated Parmesan cheese
  • 1 cup shredded mozzarella cheese
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • Fresh basil leaves for garnish

How To Do It

Step 1
Heat olive oil in a large pot over medium heat until shimmering. Add the ground beef and cook, breaking it up with a spoon, until browned and no longer pink, about 6-8 minutes. This step is crucial because browning the beef develops deep flavor through the Maillard reaction, which adds richness to the soup. Avoid stirring too often so the meat can brown properly instead of steaming. Once browned, drain excess fat if needed to prevent greasiness.
⏱ Time: 8
✓ Doneness: Beef is evenly browned with no pink spots, and you smell a rich, savory aroma.
Step 2
Add chopped onion and cook until softened and translucent, about 4 minutes. Then stir in minced garlic, oregano, basil, and red pepper flakes if using, cooking for another minute until fragrant. This allows the aromatics to release their flavors into the fat, building a flavorful base for the soup. Don’t rush this step; cooking the garlic too long can make it bitter, so watch closely and stir frequently.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Onion is soft and translucent; garlic aroma is fragrant but not burnt.
Step 3
Pour in the beef broth and crushed tomatoes, stirring to combine. Bring the mixture to a gentle boil. This step is important because simmering the broth with tomatoes and spices allows the flavors to meld and deepen. Avoid boiling vigorously to prevent the broth from reducing too quickly.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Broth is bubbling gently with a rich red color and a savory aroma.
Step 4
Add the broken lasagna noodles to the pot and stir to submerge them in the broth. Reduce heat to medium-low and simmer uncovered, stirring occasionally, until the noodles are tender but not mushy, about 10-12 minutes. Cooking the noodles directly in the broth lets them absorb the soup’s flavor, making every bite delicious. Be careful not to overcook, or the noodles will become too soft and lose texture.
⏱ Time: 12
✓ Doneness: Noodles are tender with a slight bite, and the soup has thickened slightly.
Step 5
Stir in grated Parmesan cheese and season with salt and pepper to taste. The cheese melts into the soup, adding creaminess and umami. Taste and adjust seasoning carefully, since Parmesan adds saltiness. This finishing step rounds out the flavors and adds richness.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Soup is creamy and well-seasoned, with cheese fully incorporated.
Step 6
Ladle the soup into bowls and sprinkle shredded mozzarella cheese on top. Let the heat from the soup melt the cheese slightly before serving. The melted mozzarella adds a gooey, comforting texture that’s essential to the lasagna experience in soup form.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Cheese is soft and melting, slightly stringy when lifted with a spoon.
Step 7
Garnish with fresh basil leaves for a bright herbal note and a pop of color. This fresh garnish balances the richness of the soup and adds a lovely aroma just before serving.
⏱ Time: 1
✓ Doneness: Bright green basil leaves sit fresh on top without wilting.
Step 8
Serve immediately with crusty garlic bread or a simple green salad for a complete meal. The bread is perfect for dipping and soaking up the flavorful broth.
⏱ Time: 1
✓ Doneness: Soup is hot and ready to enjoy with accompaniments.
Step 9
Store any leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge. When reheating, add a splash of broth or water to loosen the soup as the pasta will absorb liquid over time. Reheat gently on the stovetop or in the microwave.
⏱ Time: 1
✓ Doneness: Soup is reheated evenly without drying out.

Pro Tips

Brown the beef without stirring too much to develop a deep flavor crust.
Allowing the beef to brown undisturbed encourages the Maillard reaction, creating complex flavor compounds that enrich the soup. Stirring constantly prevents browning and leads to a bland, steamed texture.
Break the lasagna noodles into bite-sized pieces before adding to the soup.
Smaller pieces cook evenly and absorb the broth better, making the soup easier to eat and more flavorful. Whole sheets would take longer to cook and be cumbersome in a soup.
Add the Parmesan cheese off the heat to avoid graininess.
High heat can cause Parmesan to clump or become gritty. Stirring it in after removing the pot from heat ensures smooth melting and a creamy texture.
Simmer the soup gently to prevent noodles from overcooking.
A gentle simmer cooks the pasta evenly without breaking it down. Boiling vigorously can make noodles mushy and cause the broth to reduce excessively, altering the soup’s balance.
Use fresh basil as a garnish for a bright flavor contrast.
Adding basil at the end preserves its fresh aroma and vibrant color, balancing the rich, savory flavors of the soup and enhancing presentation.
Serve with crusty garlic bread for dipping.
The bread soaks up the flavorful broth and adds a satisfying crunchy texture, making the meal more filling and enjoyable.
Add red pepper flakes if you like a subtle spicy kick.
A small amount of heat enhances the tomato and beef flavors without overpowering the dish, adding depth and warmth.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overcooking the noodles until mushy

Why it happens Many cooks leave the noodles in boiling soup too long, thinking they need to be very soft, but this ruins texture and makes the soup gummy.

Fix Cook noodles just until al dente by checking frequently and removing from heat promptly. The noodles will continue softening slightly off the heat.

Skipping browning the beef

Why it happens Rushing to add all ingredients at once can lead to grey, steamed meat lacking flavor.

Fix Brown the beef in batches if needed to avoid crowding the pan, ensuring good caramelization and flavor development.

Using pre-grated Parmesan cheese

Why it happens Pre-grated cheese often contains anti-caking agents that prevent smooth melting.

Fix Grate fresh Parmesan yourself for best melting and flavor.

Boiling the soup too hard after adding noodles

Why it happens High heat breaks down pasta and reduces broth too quickly.

Fix Reduce heat to a gentle simmer once noodles are added and stir occasionally.

Adding fresh basil too early

Why it happens Cooking basil for too long turns it bitter and dulls its flavor.

Fix Add fresh basil just before serving to preserve its bright taste and color.

Variations

Vegetarian Lasagna Soup

Skip the beef and add diced mushrooms and zucchini for a hearty vegetarian version. Use vegetable broth instead of beef broth. The mushrooms provide umami richness that mimics the meatiness, and zucchini adds fresh texture.

Note: Sauté mushrooms and zucchini with onions before adding broth to develop flavor.

Spicy Lasagna Soup

Add extra red pepper flakes and a splash of hot sauce to the broth for a spicy kick. You can also include diced jalapeños with the aromatics. This variation adds warmth and depth for spice lovers.

Note: Adjust spice level gradually to avoid overpowering the other flavors.

Creamy Lasagna Soup

Stir in 1/2 cup heavy cream or half-and-half at the end for a richer, creamier broth. This makes the soup feel indulgent and smooth, similar to a creamy tomato bisque.

Note: Add cream off the heat and stir gently to avoid curdling.

Slow Cooker Lasagna Soup

Combine all ingredients except pasta and cheese in a slow cooker and cook on low for 6 hours. Add broken noodles in the last 30 minutes. This hands-off method is great for busy days.

Note: Check noodle doneness carefully to prevent overcooking in the slow cooker.

Gluten-Free Lasagna Soup

Use gluten-free lasagna noodles or substitute with gluten-free pasta shapes. Ensure broth and other ingredients are gluten-free. This makes the recipe accessible for gluten-sensitive eaters.

Note: Cook gluten-free pasta separately if it tends to break down quickly in soup.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Lasagna soup can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours covered. Beyond that, bacteria can grow, so refrigerate promptly. Cover the pot or bowl with a lid or plastic wrap to prevent contamination.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The pasta will absorb broth and thicken soup, so stir in a splash of broth or water when reheating. The flavor remains delicious but the texture is best fresh.

Freezer

Freeze soup in airtight containers for up to 3 months. Avoid freezing with cheese on top; add fresh cheese when reheating. Thaw in the refrigerator overnight before reheating gently on stovetop.

Reheating

Reheat gently in a saucepan over medium-low heat, stirring occasionally. Add a splash of broth or water to loosen the soup and prevent sticking. Avoid boiling vigorously to keep noodles intact and prevent separation of cheese.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the base soup (meat, broth, tomatoes, aromatics) 1-2 days ahead and refrigerate. Add noodles and cheese just before reheating and serving to keep pasta texture fresh and cheese melty.

FAQ

Can I use regular pasta instead of lasagna noodles?
Yes, you can substitute broken lasagna noodles with other pasta shapes like penne or rigatoni. Just adjust cooking time as smaller pasta may cook faster. The key is to use pasta that holds up well in soup.
How do I prevent the noodles from getting mushy?
Cook the noodles just until al dente, and keep the soup at a gentle simmer rather than a rolling boil. Also, avoid letting the soup sit too long with pasta in it before serving, as noodles will continue to absorb liquid and soften.
Can I make this soup vegetarian?
Absolutely! Replace ground beef with diced mushrooms, zucchini, or plant-based meat alternatives. Use vegetable broth instead of beef broth to keep it vegetarian while maintaining rich flavor.
Is it okay to freeze lasagna soup?
You can freeze the soup without the cheese toppings. Freeze in airtight containers for up to 3 months. Add fresh mozzarella and Parmesan when reheating for best texture and flavor.
What if I don’t have fresh basil?
Dried basil can be added during cooking, but fresh basil added just before serving gives the best bright flavor and aroma. If you don’t have fresh, omit it or substitute with fresh parsley.
Can I use ground turkey or chicken instead of beef?
Yes, ground turkey or chicken can be used for a leaner option. Brown it carefully to avoid drying out, and consider adding a bit of olive oil for moisture.
How spicy is this soup? Can I adjust the heat?
This recipe is mild by default. You can add red pepper flakes or hot sauce to taste if you like a little heat. Start with a small amount and increase gradually.
What’s the best way to reheat leftovers?
Reheat gently on the stovetop over medium-low heat, adding a splash of broth or water to loosen the soup. Avoid microwaving at high power to prevent uneven heating and drying out.
