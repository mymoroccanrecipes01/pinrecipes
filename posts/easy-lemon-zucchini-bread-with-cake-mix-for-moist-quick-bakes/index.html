Easy Lemon Zucchini Bread With Cake Mix for Moist Quick Bakes

This lemon zucchini bread made with cake mix is a quick and easy way to enjoy a moist, flavorful treat. The cake mix keeps it super tender, while fresh zucchini and lemon zest brighten every bite. Perfect for breakfast or an afternoon snack!

Introduction

I stumbled upon this lemon zucchini bread recipe years ago when I needed a quick, reliable way to use up zucchini from my garden. Using a cake mix as the base was a game changer — it keeps the bread wonderfully moist and tender without any fuss. Over time, I’ve tweaked it by adding fresh lemon zest and juice to brighten the flavor, which really makes it pop. What I love most is how easy it is to whip up on a busy afternoon, and it always impresses family and friends. The secret is squeezing out the zucchini well so the bread stays light and not soggy, and the little lemon glaze on top adds a perfect touch of sweetness and zing. When it comes out of the oven, it has a golden crust with a soft, moist crumb speckled with green zucchini bits and bursts of lemon flavor. It’s just the right balance of sweet and fresh, making it perfect for breakfast, snack time, or even a light dessert. I usually bake it in a simple loaf pan, and it slices beautifully for sharing. This recipe has become a staple in my kitchen because it’s reliable, delicious, and uses ingredients I always have on hand. I think you’ll love how easy and satisfying it is to make!

What You'll Need

1 loaf, approximately 8 servings

  • 1 box yellow cake mix (15.25 oz)
  • 1 1/2 cups grated zucchini (about 1 medium zucchini), squeezed dry
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1/3 cup vegetable oil
  • 1/4 cup fresh lemon juice
  • 1 tablespoon lemon zest
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar (for glaze)
  • 2 tablespoons lemon juice (for glaze)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by prepping the zucchini: grate it finely, then place it in a clean kitchen towel or cheesecloth and squeeze out as much moisture as possible. This step is crucial because excess water can make the bread soggy and affect its texture. Removing the moisture ensures the bread stays tender but not wet. Take your time here and press firmly to get rid of the liquid. Once dry, set the zucchini aside.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels dry to the touch and no dripping liquid remains when squeezed.
Step 2
In a large mixing bowl, combine the yellow cake mix, baking powder, and salt. Whisk these dry ingredients together to ensure even distribution, which helps the bread rise evenly and have a consistent crumb. This also prevents pockets of baking powder or salt in the loaf. Mixing the dry ingredients well at this stage is key to a balanced flavor and texture.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are uniformly blended with no clumps or uneven color.
Step 3
In a separate bowl, whisk together the eggs, vegetable oil, fresh lemon juice, lemon zest, and vanilla extract until fully combined. The eggs provide structure, the oil adds moisture, and the lemon juice and zest bring fresh brightness. Whisking them well ensures the wet ingredients emulsify, which helps create a tender crumb and even lemon flavor throughout the bread.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients are smooth and homogenous with visible lemon zest evenly dispersed.
Step 4
Add the wet ingredients to the dry mix and stir gently with a spatula until just combined. Avoid overmixing here because it can develop gluten in the cake mix flour and make the bread tough. The batter should be thick but moist, with no large pockets of dry flour. Fold in the grated zucchini last, distributing it evenly without breaking down the shreds.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Batter is thick, moist, and uniform with zucchini evenly spread.
Step 5
Pour the batter into a greased or parchment-lined 9x5-inch loaf pan, smoothing the top gently with a spatula. Even spreading helps the bread bake uniformly and prevents doming or cracking. Tap the pan lightly on the counter to release any air bubbles trapped inside the batter, which can cause holes in the crumb.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Batter surface is smooth and even, with no large air bubbles visible.
Step 6
Bake the bread in a preheated 350°F (175°C) oven for 45-50 minutes. Start checking at 45 minutes by inserting a toothpick into the center — it should come out mostly clean with a few moist crumbs. Baking too long can dry out the bread, while underbaking leaves it gummy inside. The top should be golden brown and slightly cracked.
⏱ Time: 45
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted near the center comes out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter.
Step 7
Remove the bread from the oven and let it cool in the pan for about 10 minutes. Cooling allows the bread to set and makes it easier to remove from the pan without breaking. After 10 minutes, carefully transfer the loaf to a wire rack to cool completely. This prevents condensation and sogginess on the bottom crust.
⏱ Time: 15
✓ Doneness: Bread is cool enough to handle and no longer steaming.
Step 8
While the bread cools, prepare the lemon glaze by whisking together powdered sugar and lemon juice until smooth and pourable but not too runny. The glaze adds a sweet and tangy finish that complements the zucchini bread’s flavors perfectly. Adjust the consistency by adding a bit more powdered sugar or lemon juice as needed.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Glaze is smooth, glossy, and thick enough to drizzle but not run off immediately.
Step 9
Once the bread is completely cool, drizzle the lemon glaze evenly over the top. Let it set for 10-15 minutes before slicing. The glaze will harden slightly, adding a beautiful shine and a burst of lemony sweetness to every bite.
⏱ Time: 15
✓ Doneness: Glaze is set but still glossy, not sticky or runny.

Pro Tips

Always squeeze the grated zucchini to remove excess moisture before adding it to the batter.
Excess water in zucchini can make the bread dense and soggy. Removing moisture ensures the bread stays light and tender with a perfect crumb.
Use fresh lemon zest and juice rather than bottled for the best bright citrus flavor.
Fresh lemon contains essential oils and natural acidity that enhance flavor and react with baking powder for better rise and texture.
Do not overmix the batter once wet and dry ingredients are combined.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which can make the bread tough and chewy instead of soft and tender.
Allow the bread to cool completely before drizzling the lemon glaze.
If the bread is warm, the glaze will melt and run off, losing its glossy finish and sweetness.
Tap the filled loaf pan gently on the counter before baking to release air bubbles.
This prevents large holes or tunnels in the bread crumb, resulting in a more even texture.
Use a toothpick to test doneness starting at 45 minutes to avoid overbaking.
Overbaking dries out the bread, so checking early ensures it stays moist and tender.
Store the bread wrapped tightly to preserve moisture if not serving immediately.
Proper storage keeps the bread fresh and prevents it from drying out over time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing out zucchini moisture

Why it happens Many cooks underestimate how much water zucchini holds and skip this step to save time.

Fix Always grate and then squeeze the zucchini firmly in a clean towel or cheesecloth before adding it to the batter to prevent soggy bread.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens It’s tempting to mix thoroughly to combine ingredients, but this develops gluten.

Fix Mix just until ingredients are combined and no dry flour remains to keep the bread tender.

Using bottled lemon juice

Why it happens Convenience leads some to use bottled juice, which lacks fresh citrus oils.

Fix Use fresh lemon juice and zest for vibrant flavor and proper acid balance.

Baking at too high a temperature

Why it happens Some ovens run hot or cooks increase temperature to speed baking.

Fix Bake at 350°F for even cooking; higher temps can cause crust to burn before inside cooks.

Glazing warm bread

Why it happens In excitement, glaze is added immediately after baking.

Fix Let bread cool completely to set the glaze properly and avoid it melting off.

Variations

Gluten-Free Lemon Zucchini Bread

Swap the cake mix for a gluten-free yellow cake mix to accommodate gluten sensitivities. The texture will be slightly different but still moist and flavorful with the lemon and zucchini.

Note: Gluten-free mixes sometimes require extra moisture; monitor batter consistency and add a splash of milk if too thick.

Lemon Poppy Seed Zucchini Bread

Add 2 tablespoons of poppy seeds to the batter for a delightful crunch and nutty flavor that pairs beautifully with lemon and zucchini.

Note: Fold poppy seeds in with the zucchini to distribute evenly without overmixing.

Cream Cheese Glaze Topping

Replace the lemon glaze with a cream cheese glaze made from softened cream cheese, powdered sugar, and lemon juice for a richer finish.

Note: Ensure the bread is fully cooled before applying to prevent melting.

Whole Wheat Cake Mix Version

Use a whole wheat or wheat blend cake mix for a nuttier, heartier bread. The texture will be denser but still moist thanks to the zucchini.

Note: Whole wheat absorbs more liquid; consider adding an extra tablespoon of oil if batter feels too thick.

Add Chopped Nuts

Fold in 1/2 cup of chopped walnuts or pecans for added texture and flavor contrast.

Note: Toast nuts lightly before adding to enhance their aroma and crunch.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the bread wrapped tightly in plastic wrap or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days. Keep it away from direct sunlight or heat to maintain moisture and freshness.

Refrigerator

Keep the bread in an airtight container or wrapped well in the refrigerator for up to 5 days. The bread may firm up slightly but will stay moist. Bring to room temperature or warm gently before serving.

Freezer

Wrap the bread tightly in plastic wrap and then foil to freeze for up to 3 months. To thaw, place in the refrigerator overnight or at room temperature for a few hours. Reheat slices in a toaster oven or microwave for best texture.

Reheating

Reheat slices in a toaster oven at 300°F for 5-7 minutes or microwave for 20-30 seconds. Adding a light brush of butter before warming can enhance flavor and moisture. Avoid overheating to prevent drying out.

Make Ahead

Prepare the batter a day ahead and store it covered in the refrigerator. Bake fresh the next day for best rise and texture. Alternatively, bake the bread fully and freeze it for later use.

FAQ

Can I use a different cake mix flavor?
Yellow cake mix works best because its mild flavor complements lemon and zucchini. Chocolate or other flavors may overpower the delicate citrus and vegetable notes, but you can experiment if you prefer.
Do I need to peel the zucchini?
No need to peel unless the skin is very thick or waxed. The skin adds color and nutrients. Just wash thoroughly before grating.
Can I freeze the baked bread?
Yes, wrap tightly in plastic and foil to prevent freezer burn. Thaw slowly in the refrigerator or at room temperature for best results.
Can I substitute oil with applesauce?
You can replace some or all of the oil with unsweetened applesauce for a lower-fat version, but expect a slightly different texture and less richness.
How do I make the glaze thicker or thinner?
Add more powdered sugar to thicken or more lemon juice to thin the glaze. Adjust until you get a consistency that drizzles but doesn’t run off too quickly.
Can I make muffins instead of a loaf?
Yes, bake the batter in a greased muffin tin at 350°F for about 20-25 minutes. Check doneness with a toothpick.
Why does my bread crack on top?
Cracking is normal and happens as the bread rises and the crust sets. It adds rustic charm and doesn’t affect flavor.
