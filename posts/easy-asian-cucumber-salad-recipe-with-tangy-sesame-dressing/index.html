Easy Asian Cucumber Salad Recipe with Tangy Sesame Dressing

This Asian cucumber salad is crisp, refreshing, and bursting with tangy sesame flavor. The secret is a quick marinade with rice vinegar and toasted sesame oil that makes the cucumbers irresistibly juicy. Perfect for a light lunch or side dish on warm days.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this Asian cucumber salad a few years ago while craving something light and refreshing that still packed a punch of flavor. What makes this version stand out is the balance between the tangy rice vinegar and the nutty toasted sesame oil, which gives the cucumbers an irresistible shine and depth. I remember the first time I made it, I was worried the cucumbers might get soggy, but the quick marinade keeps them crisp and juicy. Plus, the chili flakes add just the right amount of heat without overpowering the freshness. This salad is perfect for those warm days when you want something simple yet satisfying, or as a crunchy side for grilled meats. I love how easy it is to whip up, and it always disappears fast at family dinners. If you’re looking for a fuss-free salad that feels homemade but tastes like you spent hours on it, this recipe is for you!

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 2 large cucumbers, thinly sliced
  • 2 tablespoons rice vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon toasted sesame oil
  • 1 teaspoon soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon red chili flakes (adjust to taste)
  • 1 tablespoon toasted sesame seeds
  • 2 tablespoons fresh cilantro, chopped

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by washing and thinly slicing the cucumbers. Using a mandoline slicer helps achieve uniform slices, but a sharp knife works fine too. Thin slices ensure the dressing clings well and the cucumbers stay crisp. Avoid slicing too thick as it can make the salad less refreshing and harder to eat.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Cucumber slices are uniformly thin and slightly translucent.
Step 2
Place the sliced cucumbers in a colander and sprinkle with salt. Toss gently and let them sit for 10 minutes to draw out excess moisture. This step prevents the salad from becoming watery and helps the cucumbers stay crunchy when dressed.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers release some water and feel slightly less firm but not mushy.
Step 3
Meanwhile, whisk together rice vinegar, toasted sesame oil, soy sauce, sugar, and red chili flakes in a small bowl until sugar dissolves. This dressing is the heart of the salad, balancing acidity, nuttiness, sweetness, and heat for a complex flavor profile.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Dressing is smooth, well combined, and glossy.
Step 4
Pat the cucumbers dry with paper towels to remove excess moisture. Transfer them to a large mixing bowl. This prevents dilution of the dressing and keeps the salad crisp.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers feel dry to the touch but remain fresh.
Step 5
Pour the dressing over the cucumbers and toss gently but thoroughly to coat every slice. Use a light hand to avoid bruising the cucumbers. Proper tossing ensures every bite is flavorful and balanced.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers glisten with dressing, evenly coated without pooling liquid.
Step 6
Sprinkle toasted sesame seeds and chopped fresh cilantro over the salad. These add texture, aroma, and a fresh herbal note that brightens the dish.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Sesame seeds and cilantro are evenly scattered on top.
Step 7
Let the salad rest in the fridge for at least 10 minutes before serving. This allows the flavors to meld and the cucumbers to soak up the dressing without losing their crunch.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Salad is chilled and flavors taste more integrated.
Step 8
Give the salad a quick toss before serving and adjust seasoning if needed. Sometimes a pinch more salt or a splash of vinegar brightens the final taste.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Salad tastes balanced with a fresh, tangy, and slightly spicy profile.
Step 9
Serve chilled as a side dish or light snack. It pairs wonderfully with grilled chicken, rice bowls, or as a refreshing palate cleanser during a meal.
⏱ Time: 0
✓ Doneness: Salad is cool, crisp, and flavorful on the plate.

Pro Tips

Use a mandoline slicer for perfectly thin cucumber slices.
Uniform thin slices allow the dressing to coat evenly and ensure a crisp, refreshing texture. Irregular or thick slices can result in uneven flavor absorption and a less pleasant mouthfeel.
Don’t skip the salting step to draw out excess water.
Salting cucumbers prevents the salad from becoming watery and soggy by removing excess moisture. It also concentrates the cucumber’s flavor, making the salad more vibrant.
Toast your own sesame seeds for extra aroma.
Freshly toasted sesame seeds release oils and aromas that enhance the salad’s flavor. Pre-toasted seeds can lose potency over time, so toasting them yourself ensures maximum impact.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the salting step

Why it happens Many cooks overlook this step thinking it’s unnecessary or time-consuming, but it’s critical to prevent sogginess.

Fix Make sure to salt the cucumbers and let them sit for at least 10 minutes, then pat dry thoroughly before dressing to keep the salad crisp.

Using too much sesame oil

Why it happens Because toasted sesame oil has a strong flavor, it’s tempting to add a lot, but this can overpower the salad.

Fix Measure carefully and add just enough to impart aroma without drowning the other flavors. A little goes a long way.

Variations

Spicy Peanut Asian Cucumber Salad

Add a tablespoon of peanut butter to the dressing and top the salad with crushed peanuts for a creamy, nutty twist with extra texture and flavor.

Note: Whisk peanut butter into the dressing thoroughly to avoid clumps; thin with a splash of water if needed.

Miso Sesame Cucumber Salad

Incorporate a teaspoon of white miso paste into the dressing for a deeper umami flavor that complements the fresh cucumbers beautifully.

Note: Dissolve miso paste fully in the vinegar before adding oil to ensure smooth dressing.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This salad is best served fresh and should not be kept at room temperature for more than 2 hours. Cover it loosely if you need to leave it out briefly.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 2 days. The cucumbers may soften slightly but will still retain good flavor. Serve chilled for best texture.

Freezer

Not recommended to freeze as cucumbers become watery and mushy upon thawing.

Reheating

This salad is served cold and should not be reheated.

Make Ahead

Prepare the dressing and slice cucumbers separately up to a day ahead. Combine and toss just before serving to maintain crispness.

FAQ

Can I use regular white vinegar instead of rice vinegar?
You can substitute white vinegar, but rice vinegar is milder and less acidic, which better complements the delicate cucumber flavor. If using white vinegar, reduce the quantity slightly to avoid overpowering the salad.
How long can I store the salad after making it?
For best texture and flavor, consume within 2 days when stored in the refrigerator. The cucumbers will gradually soften and release water, which can dilute the dressing.
Can I add other vegetables to this salad?
Definitely! Thinly sliced radishes, carrots, or bell peppers add color and crunch. Just be mindful of their water content to keep the salad crisp.
What if I don’t have toasted sesame oil?
You can use regular sesame oil or a neutral oil like vegetable oil, but toasted sesame oil provides a distinctive nutty aroma that’s key to the authentic flavor. If using plain oil, consider adding a small handful of toasted sesame seeds for aroma.
Is this salad spicy?
The red chili flakes add a gentle heat that can be adjusted to taste. You can omit or reduce them if you prefer a milder salad.
Can I make this salad vegan?
Yes! This recipe is naturally vegan as it contains no animal products. Just ensure your soy sauce is vegan, as some brands use additives.
Why is salting the cucumbers important?
Salting helps draw out excess water from cucumbers, preventing the salad from becoming watery and enhancing the crunchiness by concentrating flavors.
Can I prepare this salad ahead for a party?
You can prepare the cucumbers and dressing separately a day ahead, then toss just before serving to keep the salad fresh and crisp.
