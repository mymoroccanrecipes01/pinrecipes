Easy Gluten Free Zucchini Bread Muffins with Moist Tender Crumb

These gluten free zucchini bread muffins are a moist, tender treat that’s perfect for breakfast or an afternoon snack. I love how the zucchini keeps them super soft without any gluten. Great for busy mornings or a cozy weekend bake!

Introduction

I first made these gluten free zucchini bread muffins a few years ago when a friend asked for a treat she could enjoy without gluten. I’d always loved classic zucchini bread but wanted to make it more accessible and a bit healthier. After some trial and error, I found the perfect balance using a blend of gluten free flour and almond flour which keeps the crumb tender and moist without being dense. The trick I learned was to squeeze out excess moisture from the zucchini but not over-dry it, so the muffins stay soft but don’t get soggy. I also add a touch of Greek yogurt for extra moisture and a subtle tang that balances the sweetness. These muffins bake up golden with a lightly crisp top and a tender, flavorful crumb flecked with zucchini. They’re perfect for breakfast, a snack, or even a light dessert. I love making a batch on the weekend to have on hand all week long. Plus, they freeze beautifully if you want to prep ahead. If you’re new to gluten free baking, this recipe is a great place to start — it’s forgiving and sure to please everyone, even those who don’t need to avoid gluten!

What You'll Need

Approximately 12 muffins

  • 2 cups grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 1 1/2 cups gluten free all-purpose flour blend
  • 1/2 cup almond flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup coconut sugar
  • 1/3 cup melted coconut oil
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/4 cup plain Greek yogurt

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and line a muffin tin with paper liners or grease well. Grate the zucchini and then place it in a clean kitchen towel or cheesecloth. Squeeze firmly to remove excess moisture but don’t overdo it; you want the zucchini damp enough to keep the muffins moist but not soggy. This step is crucial because too much water will make the muffins dense and gummy, while too little will dry them out.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels damp but not dripping wet when squeezed.
Step 2
In a large bowl, whisk together the gluten free all-purpose flour, almond flour, baking soda, baking powder, salt, cinnamon, and nutmeg. Mixing the dry ingredients first ensures even distribution of leavening agents and spices, which helps the muffins rise evenly and have consistent flavor.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are combined uniformly with no clumps.
Step 3
In a separate bowl, beat the eggs with the coconut sugar until well combined and slightly fluffy. Then add the melted coconut oil, vanilla extract, and Greek yogurt. Mixing the wet ingredients separately helps create an emulsion that traps air, contributing to a lighter muffin texture.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Wet mixture is smooth and slightly thickened.
Step 4
Pour the wet ingredients into the dry ingredients and gently fold together using a spatula. Be careful not to overmix; just combine until no dry flour streaks remain. Overmixing can develop the gluten substitutes too much, leading to a tough texture.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Batter looks uniform with visible zucchini strands.
Step 5
Fold in the grated zucchini carefully, distributing it evenly throughout the batter. The zucchini adds moisture and a subtle freshness that balances the warm spices.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Zucchini pieces are evenly incorporated without clumping.
Step 6
Spoon the batter into the muffin tin, filling each cup about 3/4 full to allow room for rising. Filling too full can cause overflow and uneven baking, while too little results in small, dry muffins.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Muffin cups are evenly filled with batter.
Step 7
Bake in the preheated oven for 25-30 minutes, rotating the pan halfway through for even baking. Start checking at 25 minutes by inserting a toothpick into the center of a muffin; it should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs.
⏱ Time: 25
✓ Doneness: Golden brown tops and toothpick comes out clean.
Step 8
Let the muffins cool in the pan for 5 minutes, then transfer to a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling prevents sogginess and helps the crumb set properly.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Muffins feel firm but tender when touched.
Step 9
Optionally, dust the tops with powdered sugar or brush with melted butter before serving for extra flavor and shine. This finishing touch adds visual appeal and a little extra richness.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Muffins have a glossy, inviting surface.

Pro Tips

Always squeeze out excess water from the zucchini but keep it slightly moist.
Zucchini contains a lot of water which can make muffins soggy if not removed. However, removing too much moisture will dry out the muffins. Squeezing gently ensures the right balance of moisture for a tender crumb.
Use room temperature ingredients for even mixing.
Room temperature eggs, yogurt, and oil blend more smoothly, creating a uniform batter. Cold ingredients can cause the batter to curdle or mix unevenly, affecting texture and rise.
Do not overmix the batter once wet and dry ingredients are combined.
Overmixing develops the structure too much, especially with gluten free flours, leading to dense or tough muffins. Fold gently just until combined for a light, tender crumb.
Rotate muffin pan halfway through baking for even cooking.
Oven heat can be uneven, causing some muffins to brown faster. Rotating the pan ensures all muffins bake evenly with consistent texture and color.
Let muffins cool completely before storing.
Cooling prevents condensation inside storage containers, which can cause muffins to become soggy or mold quickly. Proper cooling preserves texture and freshness.
Add a sprinkle of cinnamon sugar on top before baking for a crispy crust.
This adds a sweet, slightly crunchy topping that contrasts nicely with the soft crumb and enhances the muffins’ flavor and appearance.
Freeze leftover muffins individually wrapped for longer storage.
Freezing preserves freshness and texture. Wrapping individually prevents freezer burn and allows you to thaw only what you need without waste.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing enough water from zucchini.

Why it happens Many assume all moisture should be removed, but some water is necessary for moist muffins. Too much water causes sogginess and poor structure.

Fix Squeeze the zucchini firmly but stop when it feels damp, not dripping. Use a clean towel or cheesecloth for best results.

Overmixing the batter.

Why it happens Eager to combine ingredients fully, cooks often stir too vigorously, which can toughen gluten free muffins.

Fix Mix just until the ingredients are combined and no dry flour remains. Use folding motions instead of stirring.

Using a gluten free flour blend without xanthan gum.

Why it happens Some blends lack binding agents, leading to crumbly muffins.

Fix Choose a blend with xanthan gum or add 1/4 teaspoon yourself to improve elasticity and texture.

Skipping the rotation of the muffin tin.

Why it happens Ovens often have hot spots causing uneven baking.

Fix Rotate the pan halfway through baking to ensure even color and doneness.

Storing muffins before fully cooled.

Why it happens Warm muffins trapped in containers create condensation, making them soggy.

Fix Allow muffins to cool completely on a wire rack before storing in airtight containers.

Variations

Add Chocolate Chips

Fold in 1/2 cup dairy-free or regular chocolate chips to the batter for a sweet twist. The melty chocolate pairs wonderfully with zucchini’s mild flavor, making these muffins a kid-friendly treat.

Note: Chocolate chips may slightly increase baking time; watch for doneness carefully.

Walnut Zucchini Bread Muffins

Add 1/2 cup chopped toasted walnuts for crunch and nutty flavor. Walnuts complement the moist crumb and add texture contrast.

Note: Ensure nuts are evenly distributed to avoid sinking during baking.

Dairy-Free Version

Use coconut yogurt instead of Greek yogurt and coconut oil as the fat source. This keeps the muffins moist and flavorful while accommodating dairy-free diets.

Note: Texture may be slightly different but still tender and delicious.

Spiced Pumpkin Zucchini Muffins

Add 1/2 cup pumpkin puree and increase cinnamon to 1 1/2 teaspoons for a seasonal fall variation with extra moisture and warm spices.

Note: Reduce Greek yogurt slightly to compensate for added pumpkin moisture.

Lemon Zucchini Bread Muffins

Add the zest of one lemon and 1 tablespoon lemon juice for a bright, fresh flavor twist. This variation is refreshing and perfect for spring or summer.

Note: Lemon juice adds acidity; reduce yogurt slightly if desired.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store muffins in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days. Keep them in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight to prevent drying out or spoilage.

Refrigerator

Refrigerate muffins in an airtight container for up to 5 days. They may firm up slightly but will stay moist. Warm slightly in the microwave before serving for best texture.

Freezer

Freeze muffins individually wrapped in plastic wrap and placed in a freezer-safe bag for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature, then warm in the microwave or oven.

Reheating

Reheat muffins in a microwave for 20-30 seconds or in a 325°F oven for 5-7 minutes until warmed through. Adding a small pat of butter before warming enhances moisture and flavor.

Make Ahead

You can grate the zucchini and prepare the batter a day ahead, storing it covered in the fridge. Bake the muffins fresh the next day for best texture and rise.

FAQ

Can I use regular flour instead of gluten free flour?
Yes, you can substitute regular all-purpose flour in a 1:1 ratio if gluten is not a concern. The muffins will have a slightly different texture but will still be delicious and moist.
How do I know when the muffins are done baking?
Insert a toothpick into the center of a muffin; it should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs. The tops should be golden brown and spring back lightly when pressed.
Can I make these muffins vegan?
To make vegan, replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed plus 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use dairy-free yogurt and oil. The texture may be a bit different but still tasty.
Why are my muffins dry or crumbly?
Dryness can result from overbaking, not enough moisture from zucchini, or overmixing the batter. Make sure to squeeze zucchini properly and bake just until done.
Can I add nuts or fruit to the muffins?
Absolutely! Chopped walnuts, pecans, or dried fruit like raisins or cranberries can be folded into the batter for extra flavor and texture.
How do I prevent the muffins from sticking to the pan?
Use paper liners or grease the muffin tin well with oil or butter. Allow muffins to cool slightly before removing to prevent tearing.
Can I freeze the batter before baking?
It’s best to freeze baked muffins rather than raw batter. Freezing batter can affect rise and texture. Prepare and bake fresh for best results.
What is the best way to store leftover muffins?
Store cooled muffins in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or refrigerate for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze individually wrapped muffins.

Final Thoughts

I hope you enjoy making and sharing these gluten free zucchini bread muffins as much as my family does. They’re simple, comforting, and perfect for any time of day!
