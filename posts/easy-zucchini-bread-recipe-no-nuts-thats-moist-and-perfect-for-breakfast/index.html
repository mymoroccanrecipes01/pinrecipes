Easy Zucchini Bread Recipe No Nuts That’s Moist and Perfect for Breakfast

This easy zucchini bread recipe without nuts is wonderfully moist and tender, perfect for breakfast or an afternoon snack. Using freshly grated zucchini keeps it naturally sweet and soft. It’s a simple, comforting loaf you’ll want to bake again and again!

Introduction

I’ve always loved zucchini bread, but I wanted to make a version that’s nut-free for my family since we have some allergies. This recipe is a keeper! The first time I baked it, I was surprised how moist and tender it turned out without any nuts at all. The secret is really in how you prepare the zucchini—grating it finely and squeezing out the excess moisture helps keep the bread from becoming soggy but still keeps it soft and flavorful. I also add a little Greek yogurt to boost moisture and tenderness. It’s perfect for breakfast or a cozy afternoon snack with tea. The cinnamon and nutmeg add just the right touch of warmth without overpowering the delicate zucchini flavor. Plus, it’s so easy to make—just mix everything in one bowl and bake. I love sharing this loaf with friends and family because it’s simple, comforting, and always disappears fast. If you’ve struggled with dry or crumbly zucchini bread in the past, give this no-nuts version a try—you might just have a new favorite!

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, 8 servings

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 1/4 cup plain Greek yogurt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 2 cups grated zucchini, squeezed dry

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9x5 inch loaf pan or line it with parchment paper. This ensures your bread won’t stick and will come out easily after baking. Proper oven temperature is crucial for even baking and a nice golden crust, so don’t skip preheating.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Loaf pan is evenly greased and oven is fully preheated
Step 2
In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, baking soda, baking powder, salt, cinnamon, and nutmeg. Mixing the dry ingredients first distributes the leavening agents evenly, which helps the bread rise uniformly and prevents dense spots.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are fully combined and free of lumps
Step 3
In another bowl, beat the eggs with both sugars until combined. Then add the vegetable oil, Greek yogurt, and vanilla extract, mixing well. This wet mixture binds the ingredients and adds moisture and flavor. Using Greek yogurt here helps create a tender crumb.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients are smooth and fully incorporated
Step 4
Grate the zucchini finely using a box grater or food processor, then place it in a clean kitchen towel and squeeze out as much moisture as possible. This step prevents the bread from becoming soggy while keeping it moist and tender inside.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini is finely grated and feels damp but not dripping wet
Step 5
Add the wet ingredients to the dry ingredients and stir gently until just combined. Avoid overmixing to keep the bread light and tender. Then fold in the grated zucchini evenly throughout the batter.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth with no large pockets of flour; zucchini is evenly distributed
Step 6
Pour the batter into the prepared loaf pan and smooth the top with a spatula. This ensures an even bake and a nice flat top for slicing. Tap the pan gently on the counter to release any air bubbles.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is level and fills the pan evenly
Step 7
Bake in the preheated oven for 45-50 minutes, or until a toothpick inserted into the center comes out clean or with just a few moist crumbs. Baking time can vary based on your oven, so start checking at 40 minutes.
⏱ Time: 45-50 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden brown crust with a clean toothpick test
Step 8
Remove the bread from the oven and let it cool in the pan for 10 minutes. This resting time allows the bread to set and makes it easier to remove from the pan without breaking.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread feels firm and slightly pulling away from the pan edges
Step 9
Transfer the bread to a wire rack to cool completely before slicing. Cooling prevents the bread from becoming gummy and helps the flavors meld. Once cool, slice and enjoy with butter or your favorite spread.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread is fully cooled and slices cleanly without crumbling

Pro Tips

Always squeeze out excess moisture from the grated zucchini before adding it to the batter.
Removing excess water prevents the bread from becoming too wet and soggy, which can lead to a dense, gummy texture. The moisture that remains keeps the bread tender but balanced, ensuring a perfect crumb.
Use room temperature eggs and wet ingredients for better mixing and rise.
Room temperature ingredients combine more evenly and trap air better, which helps the bread rise uniformly. Cold ingredients can cause the batter to curdle or mix unevenly, resulting in uneven texture.
Don’t overmix the batter once you combine wet and dry ingredients.
Overmixing develops gluten, which makes the bread tough and dense. Mixing just until combined keeps the crumb light and tender, which is especially important in quick breads like this one.
Bake in the center of the oven for even heat distribution.
Positioning the loaf in the center avoids hot spots that can cause uneven baking or burning on one side. This ensures the bread cooks through properly and develops an even golden crust.
Let the bread cool completely before slicing.
Cooling allows the bread’s structure to set and moisture to redistribute, preventing it from crumbling or becoming gummy when cut. It also enhances flavor as the spices and zucchini meld.
Use fresh spices for the best flavor impact.
Old or pre-ground spices lose their potency over time, resulting in a bland loaf. Freshly ground cinnamon and nutmeg add vibrant aroma and warmth that elevate the bread’s flavor.
Add a light dusting of powdered sugar on top after cooling for a pretty finish.
This adds a subtle sweetness and visual appeal without overpowering the bread. It also highlights the golden crust and makes the loaf look inviting and homemade.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing enough moisture from the zucchini

Why it happens Many cooks assume grated zucchini can be added as-is, but zucchini has high water content that can make the bread soggy.

Fix After grating, wrap the zucchini in a clean kitchen towel and squeeze firmly to remove excess water. This step is crucial to maintain the bread’s texture and prevent a dense, wet loaf.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens It’s tempting to mix thoroughly to combine everything, but overmixing develops gluten, making the bread tough.

Fix Mix just until the dry ingredients are moistened and the zucchini is evenly distributed. Stop as soon as you don’t see dry flour to keep the crumb tender.

Baking at too high a temperature

Why it happens Some try to speed up baking by increasing oven temperature, which can brown the crust too fast and leave the inside undercooked.

Fix Bake at 350°F (175°C) for even cooking. If the crust browns too quickly, tent with foil halfway through baking.

Using old baking soda or powder

Why it happens Expired leavening agents lose potency, causing the bread not to rise properly.

Fix Test your baking soda by adding a bit to vinegar and watch for fizz. Replace any old baking powder or soda to ensure proper rise.

Cutting the bread before it cools

Why it happens Impatience leads to cutting warm bread, which can crumble or become gummy.

Fix Allow the bread to cool completely on a wire rack before slicing for clean cuts and best texture.

Variations

Add Chocolate Chips

For a sweeter twist, fold 1/2 cup of semi-sweet chocolate chips into the batter just before baking. This adds pockets of melty chocolate that complement the zucchini’s mild flavor.

Note: Chocolate chips can increase moisture slightly; avoid adding extra liquid. Bake as usual and check doneness with a toothpick.

Use Whole Wheat Flour

Swap out half or all of the all-purpose flour for whole wheat flour for a nuttier flavor and added fiber. The bread will be denser but still moist and delicious.

Note: Whole wheat absorbs more liquid; you may need to add an extra tablespoon of yogurt or oil to keep the batter moist.

Add Lemon Zest

Stir in 1 tablespoon of fresh lemon zest for a bright, fresh flavor that pairs beautifully with zucchini and warm spices.

Note: Add zest with the wet ingredients for even distribution. This won’t affect baking time.

Make Mini Muffins

Pour the batter into a greased or lined mini muffin tin for bite-sized zucchini treats. Perfect for lunchboxes or parties.

Note: Bake at 350°F but reduce baking time to 18-22 minutes; check with a toothpick for doneness.

Cinnamon Sugar Topping

Before baking, sprinkle a mixture of cinnamon and sugar over the top of the batter for a crunchy, sweet crust.

Note: This adds a delightful texture contrast and a touch of extra sweetness without changing baking time.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the zucchini bread wrapped tightly in plastic wrap or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days. Keep it away from direct sunlight or heat to prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 5 days. The bread may firm up and lose some moisture, so warm slices slightly in the microwave or toaster oven before serving for best texture.

Freezer

Freeze the cooled bread wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and then foil for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator or at room temperature wrapped to retain moisture.

Reheating

Reheat slices gently in a toaster oven or microwave. Adding a small pat of butter or a light spray of water before warming helps restore moisture and flavor. Avoid overheating to prevent drying.

Make Ahead

You can grate and squeeze the zucchini a day ahead and store it in the fridge. Dry ingredients can be mixed in advance and stored in an airtight container. Combine wet and dry ingredients and bake on the day you want fresh bread for best results.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for this recipe?
Yes, but thaw and drain frozen zucchini thoroughly before using to avoid excess moisture that can make the bread soggy. Squeeze it well with a kitchen towel to remove as much water as possible.
Can I substitute the vegetable oil with butter?
You can substitute melted butter for oil in equal amounts. Butter adds a richer flavor but may make the bread slightly denser. Make sure to melt and cool the butter before mixing.
How do I know when the bread is fully baked?
Insert a toothpick into the center; it should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs. The crust should be golden brown and slightly firm to the touch.
Can I add nuts or chocolate chips to this no-nut recipe?
If allergies aren’t a concern, you can add 1/2 cup chopped nuts or chocolate chips. Fold them in gently before baking. Otherwise, keep it nut-free as intended.
Why does my zucchini bread sometimes turn out dry?
Dry bread usually results from overbaking, not removing enough zucchini moisture, or overmixing the batter. Follow the recipe timing closely and squeeze the zucchini well.
Can I make this bread gluten-free?
Yes, but you’ll need a gluten-free flour blend designed for baking. The texture may differ slightly, so consider adding a binder like xanthan gum if your blend doesn’t include it.
How should I store leftover zucchini bread?
Store leftovers wrapped tightly at room temperature for up to 2 days or refrigerate for up to 5 days. Freeze for longer storage. Always keep it airtight to maintain moisture.
