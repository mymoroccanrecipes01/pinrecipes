Easy Vegan Zucchini Brownies Recipe That’s Moist, Fudgy, and Guilt-Free

These vegan zucchini brownies are surprisingly moist and fudgy without any dairy or eggs. The zucchini adds natural moisture and a subtle veggie twist that’s totally delicious. Perfect for a cozy afternoon treat or sharing with friends!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon vegan zucchini brownies a few years ago when I wanted to bake something indulgent but healthy for my family. I was pleasantly surprised how the zucchini adds moisture without making the brownies taste veggie-like at all. What makes this recipe stand out is the balance of fudgy texture and subtle sweetness, plus it’s completely dairy and egg-free. I’ve tweaked the amount of zucchini and sugar over time to get the perfect consistency that holds together but still melts in your mouth. The coconut oil adds a lovely richness and the chocolate chips give just the right gooey pockets inside. It’s a recipe I keep coming back to, especially when I want a treat that feels a little less guilty but still hits that chocolate craving. Plus, it’s simple enough for even beginner bakers to nail on the first try. When you bake these, expect a deep chocolate aroma and a moist crumb that’s perfect with a cup of tea or coffee. I hope you love making these as much as I do!

What You'll Need

Approximately 9 brownies

  • 1 cup grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 1/2 cup coconut oil, melted
  • 1 cup brown sugar
  • 1/4 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup dairy-free chocolate chips

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by grating the zucchini finely and then squeezing out as much moisture as possible using a clean kitchen towel or cheesecloth. This step is crucial because excess water can make the brownies too wet and affect the texture. You want the zucchini moist but not dripping. Set aside the drained zucchini for now.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels damp but not wet; no liquid pools when pressed.
Step 2
In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the melted coconut oil and brown sugar until smooth and glossy. This helps dissolve the sugar and creates a rich base for the brownies. Add the vanilla extract and mix to combine. Rushing this step can leave grainy sugar bits, so take your time to get a silky texture.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth, shiny, and slightly thickened.
Step 3
Add the grated zucchini to the wet ingredients and stir until evenly distributed. The zucchini adds moisture and a subtle texture that keeps the brownies from drying out. Mixing thoroughly ensures every bite has a bit of that fudgy goodness.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Zucchini is fully incorporated with no large clumps.
Step 4
In a separate bowl, sift together the all-purpose flour, cocoa powder, baking soda, and salt. Sifting removes lumps and aerates the dry ingredients, which helps produce a tender crumb. It also ensures even distribution of the leavening agent.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are light, fluffy, and lump-free.
Step 5
Gradually fold the dry ingredients into the wet zucchini mixture using a spatula. Mix just until combined to avoid overworking the batter, which can make brownies tough. The batter should be thick, glossy, and slightly sticky.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: No visible streaks of flour; batter is thick but spreadable.
Step 6
Fold in the dairy-free chocolate chips gently. These add melty pockets of chocolate that elevate the fudgy texture and flavor. Be careful not to overmix here to keep the chips intact and evenly distributed.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Chocolate chips are evenly scattered throughout the batter.
Step 7
Pour the batter into a greased 8x8 inch baking pan lined with parchment paper. Use a spatula to spread it evenly and smooth the top. This ensures even baking and a uniform thickness for consistent texture throughout.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Batter is level and fills the pan evenly without gaps.
Step 8
Bake in a preheated oven at 350°F (175°C) for 25-30 minutes. Start checking at 25 minutes by inserting a toothpick in the center; it should come out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. Overbaking dries out the brownies, so watch carefully.
⏱ Time: 30
✓ Doneness: Edges look set and crackly; center is slightly soft but not raw.
Step 9
Allow the brownies to cool completely in the pan before cutting. Cooling helps them set and makes slicing easier without crumbling. Once cooled, cut into 9 squares and enjoy. Store leftovers in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days.
⏱ Time: 30
✓ Doneness: Brownies are firm to the touch and hold shape when sliced.

Pro Tips

Always squeeze the grated zucchini well to remove excess moisture before adding it to the batter.
Excess water in the zucchini can make the brownies too wet and prevent them from setting properly. Removing moisture ensures a fudgy but firm texture and prevents sogginess.
Use room temperature coconut oil melted gently to avoid cooking the sugar when mixing.
If the coconut oil is too hot, it can partially cook the sugar and affect the batter’s consistency, leading to uneven texture in the baked brownies.
Don’t overmix the batter once the dry ingredients are added; fold gently until just combined.
Overmixing develops gluten, which makes brownies tough and cakey instead of tender and fudgy.
Line your baking pan with parchment paper with overhang for easy removal.
This prevents sticking and makes it easier to lift the entire batch out for clean slicing.
Check brownies a few minutes before the minimum baking time to avoid overbaking.
Brownies continue to cook slightly as they cool. Overbaking dries them out and reduces the fudgy texture.
Allow brownies to cool completely before cutting.
Cooling helps the brownies set and hold together, producing neat slices without crumbling.
Use high-quality cocoa powder for the best chocolate flavor.
Lower quality cocoa can taste bitter or flat, which affects the overall taste of the brownies.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing enough moisture from the zucchini

Why it happens Many cooks underestimate how watery zucchini is and add it directly to the batter, thinking the moisture will help.

Fix Grate finely and use a clean towel or cheesecloth to press out as much liquid as possible before mixing. This prevents the batter from becoming too wet and ensures the brownies bake properly.

Overbaking the brownies

Why it happens It’s tempting to bake until a toothpick comes out clean, but this can dry out fudgy brownies.

Fix Start checking 5 minutes before the suggested time. Look for moist crumbs on the toothpick and slightly soft centers. Remove from oven promptly to keep them moist.

Using too hot coconut oil

Why it happens Melting coconut oil in the microwave or on high heat can cause it to be too hot when mixed with sugar.

Fix Melt coconut oil gently over low heat or in short bursts in the microwave. Let it cool slightly before mixing to avoid cooking the sugar.

Overmixing the batter after adding flour

Why it happens Eager to combine ingredients, some mix vigorously, developing gluten.

Fix Fold dry ingredients gently until just combined to keep brownies tender and fudgy.

Not lining the pan with parchment paper

Why it happens Skipping this step causes brownies to stick and break apart when removing.

Fix Always line your pan with parchment paper with overhang for easy removal and cleaner slices.

Variations

Nutty Vegan Zucchini Brownies

Add 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or pecans to the batter for a crunchy texture and nutty flavor that complements the rich chocolate. Toast the nuts lightly beforehand to enhance their aroma.

Note: Fold nuts in gently after adding chocolate chips to avoid breaking them. Expect a slightly denser texture with added crunch.

Gluten-Free Vegan Zucchini Brownies

Swap the all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend that includes xanthan gum. This keeps the brownies tender while accommodating gluten sensitivities.

Note: Check the blend’s instructions for substitutions and baking time adjustments; you may need to add a little extra moisture.

Spiced Vegan Zucchini Brownies

Add 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon and 1/4 teaspoon cayenne pepper for a warm, spicy kick that pairs beautifully with chocolate and zucchini.

Note: Add spices with the dry ingredients for even distribution. The cayenne adds subtle heat, so adjust to taste.

Vegan Zucchini Brownies with Espresso

Mix 1 tablespoon instant espresso powder into the wet ingredients to deepen the chocolate flavor and add a subtle coffee note that enhances richness.

Note: Espresso powder dissolves best in warm liquids, so add it to the melted coconut oil and sugar mixture.

Vegan Zucchini Brownies with Coconut Flakes

Sprinkle 1/3 cup unsweetened shredded coconut on top of the batter before baking for a tropical twist and chewy texture contrast.

Note: The coconut toasts during baking, adding flavor and crunch. Watch closely near the end to prevent burning.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store brownies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. Keep them away from direct sunlight or heat to maintain moisture and texture.

Refrigerator

Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 5 days. The brownies will firm up and may lose some softness; let them come to room temperature before serving for best texture.

Freezer

Freeze brownies individually wrapped in plastic wrap and stored in a freezer bag for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature before serving to restore texture.

Reheating

Warm brownies gently in the microwave for 10-15 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) for 5-7 minutes to revive softness. Avoid overheating as it can dry them out.

Make Ahead

You can grate the zucchini and squeeze out moisture 1-2 days ahead, storing it in the fridge. Mix and bake the batter fresh for best results, or bake ahead and store baked brownies as described.

FAQ

Can I substitute the coconut oil with another oil?
Yes, you can use other neutral oils like vegetable or canola oil, but coconut oil adds a subtle richness and helps create a fudgy texture. Using butter is not vegan and changes the flavor and texture.
Do the zucchini brownies taste like vegetables?
Not at all! The zucchini adds moisture and texture but its flavor is very mild and masked by the rich chocolate. It’s a great way to sneak some veggies into a dessert without noticeable taste.
Can I use gluten-free flour in this recipe?
Absolutely! Use a gluten-free all-purpose blend with xanthan gum for best results. The texture might be slightly different but still delicious and fudgy.
How do I know when the brownies are done baking?
Check by inserting a toothpick in the center; it should come out with moist crumbs but no wet batter. The edges will look set and slightly crackly.
Can I add nuts or other mix-ins?
Yes, chopped nuts, coconut flakes, or vegan chocolate chunks work well. Fold them in gently after mixing the batter to avoid overmixing.
How do I store leftover brownies?
Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days or refrigerate up to 5 days. They also freeze well for up to 3 months.
Can I make these brownies without chocolate chips?
Yes, you can omit chocolate chips or replace them with chopped vegan chocolate bars. The brownies will still be fudgy and delicious.
What’s the best way to grate zucchini for this recipe?
Use the fine side of a box grater or a food processor with a grating attachment. After grating, squeeze out excess moisture with a clean towel to prevent soggy brownies.
