This easy lasagna soup is like a warm hug in a bowl, packed with hearty ground beef, tender noodles, and creamy ricotta. I love how it all comes together quickly for a cozy weeknight meal that feels special without the fuss!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this lasagna soup recipe on a chilly evening when I craved all the cozy flavors of lasagna but didn’t want to fuss with layers and baking. It’s become a quick favorite in my house because it captures everything I love about traditional lasagna — the meaty tomato sauce, tender noodles, and cheesy goodness — but in a warm, comforting soup form. What sets this version apart is the addition of creamy ricotta stirred in at the end, which gives it a luscious, velvety texture that feels indulgent but is so simple to make. I also like breaking the noodles into bite-sized pieces so they cook evenly and soak up all that flavorful broth. It’s perfect for busy weeknights when you want something hearty and satisfying without spending hours in the kitchen. The finished soup is rich and fragrant, with a beautiful balance of herbs and a fresh basil garnish that brightens every spoonful. Plus, it reheats beautifully, making it a great option for leftovers or meal prep. Honestly, it’s become my go-to comfort meal when I want something that feels a little special but is totally doable for any home cook.

What You'll Need

4 generous servings

  • 1 pound ground beef, raw
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 can (28 ounces) crushed tomatoes
  • 4 cups chicken broth
  • 8 ounces lasagna noodles, broken into pieces
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1 teaspoon dried basil
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • 1 cup ricotta cheese
  • 1 cup shredded mozzarella cheese
  • Fresh basil leaves for garnish

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by heating a large pot over medium heat. Add the ground beef along with the diced onion and minced garlic. Cook, stirring often, until the beef is nicely browned and the onions are soft and translucent. This step is crucial because browning the meat deeply develops flavor through the Maillard reaction, which you want to capture for that rich soup base. Be sure to break up the beef into small pieces as it cooks for even texture. Once browned, drain any excess fat to keep the soup from becoming greasy.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Beef is browned with no pink remaining, onions are soft and fragrant.
Step 2
Pour in the crushed tomatoes and chicken broth, then add the broken lasagna noodles, dried oregano, dried basil, salt, and pepper. Stir everything together and bring the mixture to a boil. Once boiling, reduce the heat to a gentle simmer and cook uncovered for about 20 minutes until the noodles are tender but not mushy. This slow simmer allows the flavors to meld beautifully while the noodles absorb the savory broth. Avoid rushing this step to prevent undercooked pasta or a watery soup.
⏱ Time: 20
✓ Doneness: Noodles are tender and broth has thickened slightly with rich color.
Step 3
Lower the heat and gently stir in the ricotta cheese until it’s warmed through and evenly distributed, adding a creamy richness to the soup. This finishing touch makes the soup feel indulgent without adding heaviness. Taste and adjust seasoning with salt and pepper if needed. Ladle the soup into bowls, then sprinkle shredded mozzarella on top so it melts slightly from the heat. Garnish with fresh basil leaves for a bright, herbal note that cuts through the richness.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Ricotta is smooth and incorporated; mozzarella is melted but not browned.
Step 4
Serve the soup hot with crusty garlic bread or a simple green salad on the side. The warm, cheesy, and hearty flavors make it a perfect comfort meal especially on cooler evenings. Leftovers can be stored and reheated, though the noodles may absorb more broth over time, so add extra broth or water when reheating to loosen the soup if needed.
⏱ Time: 0
✓ Doneness: Soup served steaming hot with melted cheese topping.
Step 5
If you want to prep ahead, you can brown the beef and cook the onions and garlic the day before, then refrigerate them. When ready to cook, just add the remaining ingredients and follow the simmering steps. This saves time on busy days while still delivering fresh flavor.
⏱ Time: 0
✓ Doneness: Beef mixture stored chilled and ready to combine with broth and noodles.
Step 6
For a vegetarian twist, swap the ground beef with cooked lentils or mushrooms and use vegetable broth. The mushrooms add a meaty texture and umami flavor that complements the tomato base well.
⏱ Time: 0
✓ Doneness: Soup base rich and hearty without meat.
Step 7
When breaking the lasagna noodles, try to keep pieces fairly uniform so they cook evenly. Smaller pieces cook faster and absorb more broth, which creates a thicker, more comforting texture.
⏱ Time: 0
✓ Doneness: Noodle pieces roughly the same size, about 1-2 inches each.
Step 8
Don’t skip draining the fat after browning the beef. Too much grease will make the soup heavy and oily. If you prefer, you can use lean ground beef or turkey to reduce fat content.
⏱ Time: 0
✓ Doneness: Excess fat removed, leaving a clean, flavorful base.
Step 9
Add fresh herbs like basil or parsley right before serving to keep their bright flavors and vibrant color intact. Adding them too early will cause them to wilt and lose flavor.
⏱ Time: 0
✓ Doneness: Herbs fresh and bright on top of the finished soup.

Pro Tips

Brown the beef thoroughly before adding liquids.
Browning beef creates complex flavor compounds through the Maillard reaction, which gives the soup its rich, savory base. If you skip this or don’t brown well, the soup will taste flat and lack depth.
Break lasagna noodles into uniform pieces.
Uniform noodle pieces cook evenly and absorb broth consistently. If pieces vary too much, some will overcook and turn mushy while others remain underdone, affecting overall texture.
Add ricotta cheese off heat at the end.
Adding ricotta off direct heat prevents it from curdling or separating, ensuring a smooth, creamy texture that enriches the broth without turning grainy.
Simmer noodles gently without covering.
Simmering uncovered helps thicken the broth as starches release from the noodles. Covering traps steam and keeps broth thinner, which changes the soup’s consistency.
Drain excess fat after browning beef.
Removing fat prevents the soup from becoming greasy and heavy, allowing the tomato and herb flavors to shine through more clearly.
Use fresh basil as garnish just before serving.
Fresh basil adds a bright herbal note and vibrant color that balances the rich soup. Adding it too early causes wilting and loss of flavor.
Serve with crusty garlic bread.
The crunchy bread contrasts the creamy soup and is perfect for dipping, enhancing the overall comforting meal experience.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overcooking the noodles until mushy.

Why it happens Many cooks leave the noodles simmering too long, thinking it will deepen flavor, but pasta can quickly become soggy and fall apart.

Fix Keep a close eye on the noodles and test frequently. Remove from heat as soon as they’re tender but still slightly firm to the bite (al dente). If reheating leftovers, add extra broth to loosen the noodles.

Skipping browning the beef.

Why it happens To save time, some skip this step and add raw meat directly, resulting in bland flavor and poor texture.

Fix Always brown the beef first to develop deep, savory flavors. It’s worth the extra 10 minutes and makes a big difference.

Adding ricotta too early.

Why it happens Ricotta can curdle or separate if cooked at high heat for too long.

Fix Stir ricotta in off the heat or at very low temperature just before serving to keep it creamy.

Using fresh lasagna noodles.

Why it happens Fresh noodles tend to become mushy quickly in soup.

Fix Use dried lasagna noodles broken into pieces for best texture.

Not seasoning properly.

Why it happens Relying solely on canned tomatoes and broth can make soup bland.

Fix Season with salt, pepper, and dried herbs during cooking and adjust at the end for balanced flavor.

Variations

Vegetarian Lasagna Soup

Replace ground beef with sautéed mushrooms and lentils for a hearty vegetarian version. Use vegetable broth and add extra herbs like thyme for depth. Expect a rich, earthy flavor with a similar comforting texture.

Note: Simmer mushrooms until browned to develop umami; lentils should be cooked separately or pre-cooked to avoid mushiness.

Spicy Lasagna Soup

Add crushed red pepper flakes or diced jalapeños with the garlic for a spicy kick. Top with pepper jack cheese instead of mozzarella for extra heat. This variation balances comforting flavors with a lively spice.

Note: Adjust spice level gradually to avoid overpowering the tomato base.

Slow Cooker Lasagna Soup

Brown beef and sauté aromatics first, then transfer all ingredients to a slow cooker. Cook on low for 4-6 hours, adding noodles in the last 30 minutes. This hands-off method develops deep flavors with minimal effort.

Note: Add noodles late to prevent them from overcooking and turning mushy.

Creamy White Lasagna Soup

Swap crushed tomatoes for a creamy béchamel base with spinach and chicken for a white lasagna twist. Use ricotta and mozzarella as usual. Expect a rich, velvety soup with a fresh green note.

Note: Prepare béchamel separately and combine gently to avoid curdling.

Gluten-Free Lasagna Soup

Use gluten-free lasagna noodles or substitute with broken rice noodles. Ensure broth and other ingredients are gluten-free. The texture will be slightly different but still delicious and comforting.

Note: Cook gluten-free noodles separately to control doneness and prevent overcooking.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Lasagna soup should not be left out at room temperature for more than 2 hours to avoid bacterial growth. Cover the pot or bowls loosely if serving buffet-style to keep it warm and protected.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 3 days. The noodles will absorb more broth and thicken the soup; add a splash of broth or water when reheating to loosen the texture. Reheat gently on the stovetop or microwave until steaming hot.

Freezer

Freeze the soup in airtight containers for up to 2 months. It’s best to freeze the soup without adding cheese toppings. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator and reheat on the stove, adding extra broth to restore consistency.

Reheating

Reheat on medium-low heat on the stovetop, stirring occasionally to prevent sticking. Add a little broth or water if the soup is too thick. Avoid boiling after adding cheese to prevent separation. Microwave reheating is fine but stir halfway through for even warming.

Make Ahead

You can brown the beef and sauté onions and garlic 1-2 days ahead, then refrigerate. Assemble and cook the soup fresh when ready. Alternatively, prepare the entire soup except for the cheese toppings and refrigerate overnight, adding the cheeses just before serving.

FAQ

Can I use ground turkey instead of beef?
Absolutely! Ground turkey is a leaner option and works well in this soup. Just be sure to season it well and brown it thoroughly to develop flavor. You might want to add a bit more olive oil or butter since turkey is leaner and can dry out.
What’s the best way to prevent noodles from getting mushy?
Use dried lasagna noodles broken into uniform pieces and simmer just until tender. Avoid overcooking by testing noodles frequently and removing the soup from heat as soon as they’re al dente. If reheating, add extra broth to loosen thickened noodles.
Can I make this soup vegetarian?
Yes! Swap the ground beef for sautéed mushrooms and/or cooked lentils, and use vegetable broth. The mushrooms add a meaty texture and umami flavor, making the soup hearty and satisfying without meat.
Is it okay to add ricotta cheese earlier in the cooking process?
It’s best to add ricotta at the end off the heat to prevent curdling and maintain a creamy texture. Adding it too early or cooking it at high heat can cause it to separate and become grainy.
Can I freeze lasagna soup?
Yes, you can freeze the soup without the cheese toppings for up to 2 months. Thaw it overnight in the fridge and reheat gently, adding broth if needed to restore texture. Cheese toppings are best added fresh after reheating.
What can I serve with lasagna soup?
Crusty garlic bread or a simple green salad pairs perfectly with this soup. The bread is great for dipping and adds a nice crunchy contrast to the creamy, hearty soup.
How do I store leftovers?
Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The noodles will soak up broth and thicken the soup, so add a splash of broth or water when reheating to loosen it up.
Can I prepare any steps ahead of time?
Yes, you can brown the beef and sauté the aromatics up to 2 days in advance and refrigerate. Assemble and cook the soup fresh when ready to maintain the best texture and flavor.
