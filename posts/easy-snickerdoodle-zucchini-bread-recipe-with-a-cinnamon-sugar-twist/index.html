Easy Snickerdoodle Zucchini Bread Recipe with a Cinnamon Sugar Twist

This snickerdoodle zucchini bread is a delightful twist on classic comfort baking. The cinnamon sugar swirl adds a cozy sweetness that pairs perfectly with the moist zucchini. Great for breakfast or a sweet snack anytime!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this snickerdoodle zucchini bread recipe a few summers ago when I had an abundance of zucchini from my garden and a craving for something sweet and cozy. The idea to combine the classic snickerdoodle cinnamon sugar flavor with moist zucchini bread seemed unusual at first, but the results blew me away. This version is better than your standard zucchini bread because it has that irresistible cinnamon sugar swirl baked right in, adding a perfect sweet crunch on top and pockets of warmth throughout. One of my favorite tricks is to squeeze the zucchini thoroughly to avoid soggy batter, which keeps the bread perfectly moist but not wet. Plus, I use sour cream for extra tenderness and a subtle tang that balances the sweetness. The finished bread is golden brown with a crackly cinnamon sugar crust and a tender, fluffy crumb flecked with green zucchini strands. It’s a comforting treat that feels like a warm hug in every bite. Whether you enjoy it for breakfast, a snack, or alongside your afternoon tea, this bread always brings a smile to my family’s faces. I hope you’ll love it as much as we do!

What You'll Need

One 9x5-inch loaf, approximately 8 servings

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 teaspoons ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 1/2 cups grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 1/2 cup sour cream
  • 1/4 cup cinnamon sugar (for swirling and topping)

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and flour a 9x5-inch loaf pan to ensure the bread doesn’t stick and bakes evenly. In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking soda, salt, and cinnamon. This dry mix is the foundation for your bread’s texture and flavor, so make sure it’s well combined to distribute the cinnamon evenly throughout the loaf.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are fully mixed with no lumps or clumps of cinnamon.
Step 2
In a large bowl, cream the softened butter with granulated and brown sugars until light and fluffy. This step incorporates air into the batter, which helps create a tender crumb. Beat in the eggs one at a time, then stir in the vanilla extract. The batter should look smooth and glossy at this point.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Butter and sugars are pale and fluffy; eggs fully incorporated with no streaks.
Step 3
Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture gently, alternating with the sour cream. This method prevents overmixing, which can make the bread tough. The batter will be thick and slightly lumpy. Carefully fold in the grated zucchini, making sure it’s evenly distributed without deflating the batter too much.
⏱ Time: 7
✓ Doneness: Batter is thick, moist, and speckled with shredded zucchini throughout.
Step 4
Spoon half the batter into the prepared loaf pan. Sprinkle half the cinnamon sugar evenly over the batter. Then, add the remaining batter on top and finish with the rest of the cinnamon sugar. This layering creates the signature snickerdoodle swirl and crunchy topping.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Batter in pan is evenly layered with cinnamon sugar visible on top.
Step 5
Using a butter knife or skewer, gently swirl the cinnamon sugar into the batter to create a marbled effect. Be careful not to over-swirl; you want distinct pockets of cinnamon sugar for texture and flavor contrast.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Visible swirls of cinnamon sugar throughout the batter without blending completely.
Step 6
Place the loaf pan in the preheated oven and bake for 50-60 minutes. Start checking around 50 minutes by inserting a toothpick into the center; it should come out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. Baking too long will dry out the bread, so watch closely.
⏱ Time: 50
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted comes out mostly clean with moist crumbs, crust is golden brown.
Step 7
Remove the bread from the oven and let it cool in the pan for 15 minutes. This resting time helps the bread set and makes it easier to remove from the pan without breaking.
⏱ Time: 15
✓ Doneness: Bread feels firm to the touch and slightly pulls away from pan edges.
Step 8
Carefully transfer the bread to a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling fully prevents the bread from becoming soggy and allows flavors to develop.
⏱ Time: 30
✓ Doneness: Bread is cool to the touch and holds its shape when sliced.
Step 9
Slice and serve! This bread is delicious on its own or with a smear of butter or cream cheese. Store leftovers in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Slices cut cleanly with visible moist crumb and cinnamon sugar pockets.

Pro Tips

Squeeze the zucchini well to remove excess moisture before adding it to the batter.
Excess water from zucchini can make the bread soggy and dense, preventing it from baking properly. Removing moisture ensures the right batter consistency and a tender crumb.
Cream the butter and sugars thoroughly until light and fluffy before adding eggs.
This step incorporates air into the batter, which helps the bread rise and results in a lighter, softer texture instead of a dense loaf.
Use a gentle folding technique when mixing dry ingredients and zucchini into the batter.
Overmixing develops gluten and deflates air bubbles, resulting in a tough or heavy bread. Folding maintains lightness while evenly distributing ingredients.
Don’t over-swirl the cinnamon sugar; just create gentle marbled pockets.
Overmixing cinnamon sugar into the batter will blend it completely, losing the signature snickerdoodle texture and contrast between sweet pockets and tender crumb.
Bake at 350°F and check doneness with a toothpick starting at 50 minutes.
Baking at too high a temperature can burn the sugar topping before the bread is cooked through. Checking early prevents overbaking and dryness.
Cool the bread fully on a wire rack before slicing.
Cooling allows the crumb to set and moisture to redistribute, resulting in cleaner slices and better texture.
Use fresh cinnamon for the best aroma and flavor in the cinnamon sugar.
Old or stale cinnamon loses its potency, which can make the bread less flavorful and aromatic.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not removing enough moisture from zucchini

Why it happens Many assume grated zucchini can be added directly, but it contains a lot of water that can flood the batter.

Fix After grating, place zucchini in a clean kitchen towel and squeeze firmly to remove excess liquid. This prevents soggy bread and helps it bake evenly.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens Eager cooks often mix too vigorously, trying to combine ingredients thoroughly.

Fix Use gentle folding motions just until ingredients are combined. This keeps the bread tender and prevents toughness from gluten overdevelopment.

Skipping the cinnamon sugar swirl

Why it happens Some skip this step to save time or think it’s unnecessary.

Fix Layer and swirl cinnamon sugar as instructed to get the signature snickerdoodle flavor and texture contrast that makes this bread special.

Baking at too high a temperature

Why it happens Trying to speed up baking or using inaccurate ovens can cause this.

Fix Maintain 350°F and test doneness with a toothpick starting at 50 minutes to avoid burning the crust or drying out the crumb.

Slicing bread before it’s fully cooled

Why it happens Impatience leads to cutting warm bread.

Fix Allow the bread to cool completely on a wire rack to set the crumb, ensuring clean slices and better texture.

Variations

Walnut Snickerdoodle Zucchini Bread

Add 1/2 cup chopped toasted walnuts to the batter for crunch and nutty flavor. The walnuts complement the cinnamon sugar swirl and add texture variety without overpowering the zucchini.

Note: Fold walnuts in gently after adding zucchini to avoid breaking them up and to keep the batter light.

Gluten-Free Snickerdoodle Zucchini Bread

Substitute the all-purpose flour with a gluten-free baking blend. Expect a slightly denser texture but still moist and flavorful. Make sure your blend contains xanthan gum for structure.

Note: Check doneness carefully as gluten-free breads can bake faster or slower depending on the blend.

Vegan Snickerdoodle Zucchini Bread

Replace butter with vegan margarine, eggs with flax eggs (1 tbsp ground flax + 3 tbsp water per egg), and sour cream with coconut yogurt. The bread will be slightly denser but still moist and delicious.

Note: Allow flax eggs to thicken fully before mixing; batter may be thicker and require slightly longer baking time.

Maple Cinnamon Snickerdoodle Zucchini Bread

Swap granulated sugar for pure maple syrup and add a teaspoon of ground nutmeg. This adds warmth and a subtle maple flavor that pairs beautifully with cinnamon and zucchini.

Note: Reduce sour cream slightly to balance added liquid from maple syrup.

Chocolate Chip Snickerdoodle Zucchini Bread

Stir in 1 cup mini chocolate chips for a sweet surprise. The chocolate melts into the moist crumb, adding richness alongside the cinnamon sugar swirl.

Note: Toss chips in a tablespoon of flour before folding in to prevent sinking.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the bread covered at room temperature for up to 3 days. Keep it in an airtight container or wrapped tightly in plastic wrap to prevent drying out. Avoid leaving it uncovered to maintain moisture.

Refrigerator

Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 5 days. The bread may firm up and lose some moisture, so warm slices gently in a toaster oven or microwave before serving for best texture.

Freezer

Freeze the bread wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and then foil for up to 3 months. To thaw, leave at room temperature for a few hours or overnight in the fridge. Reheat slices gently to restore softness.

Reheating

Reheat slices in a toaster oven or microwave for 15-20 seconds. Adding a small pat of butter before warming helps restore moisture and flavor. Avoid overheating to prevent drying.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter up to 1 day ahead and keep it refrigerated in the loaf pan, covered tightly with plastic wrap. Bring to room temperature before baking. Alternatively, bake the bread fully and freeze slices for quick future snacks.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for this bread?
Using frozen zucchini is possible but not ideal because it releases more water when thawed, which can make the batter soggy. If you use frozen, thaw it completely and squeeze out as much moisture as possible before adding it to the batter to maintain the right texture.
What if I don’t have sour cream? Can I substitute it?
You can substitute sour cream with plain Greek yogurt or buttermilk in equal amounts. Both provide acidity and moisture that help tenderize the crumb. Avoid using regular milk alone, as it lacks the acidity needed for proper rise and texture.
How do I know when the bread is done baking?
Insert a toothpick into the center of the loaf around 50 minutes. It should come out with a few moist crumbs but no wet batter. The crust will be golden brown and slightly crackly. If the toothpick is wet, bake a few minutes longer and check again.
Can I make this bread nut-free?
Yes, this recipe is naturally nut-free unless you add nuts as a variation. Just be sure to use nut-free cinnamon sugar and check all ingredients if allergies are a concern.
Why is my bread dense or gummy?
Common causes include too much moisture from zucchini, overmixing the batter, or underbaking. Make sure to squeeze out zucchini moisture, fold ingredients gently, and bake fully until a toothpick comes out mostly clean.
Can I double the recipe for a larger batch?
You can double the ingredients and bake in two loaf pans or a larger pan. Adjust baking time accordingly—larger pans may require longer baking. Check doneness with a toothpick at regular intervals.
What’s the best way to grate zucchini?
Use the medium holes on a box grater or a food processor with a grating attachment. After grating, place the zucchini in a clean kitchen towel and squeeze firmly to remove excess water.
Can I use a different type of flour?
All-purpose flour works best for structure and tenderness. You can try whole wheat flour for a nuttier flavor but expect a denser bread. Substitute up to half the flour with whole wheat for best results.
