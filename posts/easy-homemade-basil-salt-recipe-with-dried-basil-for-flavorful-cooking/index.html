Easy Homemade Basil Salt Recipe with Dried Basil for Flavorful Cooking

This homemade basil salt is a simple way to add fresh herb flavor to any dish. Using dried basil gives it a deep, aromatic punch. Perfect for seasoning meats, veggies, or sprinkling on salads for a quick flavor boost.

Introduction

I first started making basil salt when I wanted a quick way to add fresh herb flavor to dishes without reaching for fresh basil every time. Using dried basil is a game changer because it’s shelf-stable and super fragrant once mixed with salt. What makes this recipe special is the simple crushing technique I use to release the basil’s oils without turning it to dust, so you get those big flavorful flakes in every pinch. This basil salt is perfect for sprinkling on roasted veggies, grilled chicken, or even popcorn for a savory twist. It’s easy, versatile, and adds a lovely herbal note that fresh basil can’t deliver in the same way. Plus, it stores beautifully, so I always keep some on hand for quick seasoning. You’ll love how it brightens up your meals with minimal effort.

What You'll Need

Approximately 1 cup basil salt, about 6 servings

  • 1 cup coarse sea salt
  • 2 tablespoons dried basil leaves, crushed

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by measuring out your coarse sea salt and dried basil. Use a mortar and pestle or a spice grinder to gently crush the dried basil leaves just enough to release their aroma but not so much that they turn into powder. This step is crucial because it helps the basil infuse the salt with its flavor without losing that lovely texture. Avoid over-grinding as the flavor can become bitter and the texture less appealing.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: You’ll notice the basil leaves become slightly crushed and fragrant but still retain some of their leafy texture.
Step 2
Combine the crushed dried basil with the coarse sea salt in a bowl. Mix thoroughly with a spoon or whisk until the basil is evenly distributed throughout the salt. This ensures every pinch of salt you use will have a consistent basil flavor. If you want a finer texture, you can pulse the mixture briefly in a spice grinder, but be careful not to overdo it. Store your basil salt in an airtight container at room temperature to keep it fresh.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: The mixture should look evenly speckled with green basil flakes throughout the white salt crystals.
Step 3
Use your basil salt as a finishing seasoning on roasted vegetables, grilled meats, or even sprinkle it on fresh salads. It’s also fantastic stirred into homemade salad dressings or sprinkled over fresh mozzarella for a quick appetizer. The coarse texture gives a nice crunch and burst of herbal flavor.
⏱ Time: No cook time
✓ Doneness: When sprinkled on food, the basil salt adds a visible pop of green and an aromatic herbal note.
Step 4
If you want to intensify the basil flavor, you can let the basil salt sit sealed for a day or two before using. This resting period allows the flavors to meld and deepen, making your seasoning even more flavorful.
⏱ Time: Optional 24 hours
✓ Doneness: The aroma of basil will be stronger and more integrated into the salt crystals.
Step 5
To keep your basil salt fresh, always use a dry spoon when scooping it out. Moisture can cause clumping or spoilage over time. Store it in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight.
⏱ Time: Ongoing
✓ Doneness: Salt remains free-flowing and basil flakes stay dry and vibrant.
Step 6
For gift-giving, package your basil salt in small glass jars with airtight lids. Add a label with the date and usage suggestions. Homemade herb salts make charming and useful gifts for friends and family.
⏱ Time: No cook time
✓ Doneness: Jars are sealed airtight and labeled neatly.
Step 7
Experiment by adding other dried herbs like thyme or rosemary to create your own herb salt blends. Just keep the ratio similar so the salt remains the main seasoning base.
⏱ Time: No cook time
✓ Doneness: Herb salt blend looks visually balanced with herb flakes evenly dispersed.
Step 8
If you prefer a finer salt texture, substitute fine sea salt instead of coarse. The basil flavor will still shine but the salt will dissolve more quickly on food.
⏱ Time: No cook time
✓ Doneness: Salt texture is finer but basil flakes remain visible.
Step 9
Always taste your basil salt before adding more to dishes. Its flavor is concentrated so a little goes a long way. Adjust seasoning gradually to avoid overpowering your food.
⏱ Time: No cook time
✓ Doneness: Balanced herbal salt flavor without bitterness or saltiness overwhelming.

Pro Tips

Always crush dried basil gently by hand rather than using a blender or food processor.
Blenders and processors generate heat and tend to pulverize herbs into powder, which can release bitter compounds and lose the fresh herbal aroma. Crushing by hand preserves the leaf texture and releases essential oils gradually, resulting in a more balanced flavor.
Use coarse sea salt instead of fine salt for better texture and flavor retention.
Coarse salt crystals dissolve slowly, allowing the basil flavor to infuse gradually when seasoning food. Fine salt dissolves too quickly, muting the herb’s impact and causing the seasoning to lose potency faster.
Store basil salt in a cool, dry place away from sunlight in an airtight container.
Exposure to moisture and light degrades dried herbs and salt quality, causing clumping and loss of flavor. Airtight glass jars preserve the seasoning’s freshness and vibrant color for months.
Let the basil salt rest sealed for 24 hours before first use.
This resting period allows the flavors to meld and intensify, making the seasoning more aromatic and flavorful. It also helps the salt absorb the basil oils evenly.
Use a dry spoon to scoop basil salt to prevent moisture contamination.
Introducing moisture can cause the salt to clump and the herbs to degrade prematurely. Keeping utensils dry maintains the seasoning’s texture and shelf life.
Experiment with adding other dried herbs in small amounts for custom blends.
Adding complementary herbs like thyme or rosemary can create unique flavor profiles. However, balance is key to prevent overpowering the basil’s delicate aroma.
Taste the basil salt before seasoning dishes to gauge saltiness and herb intensity.
Basil salt can be potent, so starting with a small amount prevents over-seasoning. Adjust gradually to achieve the perfect balance for your palate.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Over-grinding the dried basil into powder

Why it happens Many cooks try to speed up the process by using electric grinders, which pulverize the basil too finely.

Fix Use a mortar and pestle or crush gently by hand to preserve leaf texture and avoid bitter flavors. This technique releases oils without turning the basil into powder.

Using fine table salt instead of coarse sea salt

Why it happens Fine salt is often more readily available and easier to mix.

Fix Choose coarse sea salt for better texture and flavor absorption. It also prevents the seasoning from clumping and losing potency.

Storing basil salt in non-airtight containers

Why it happens People often use whatever jar is handy without sealing tightly.

Fix Use airtight glass jars to keep moisture and air out, preserving freshness and preventing clumping.

Adding fresh basil instead of dried

Why it happens Some assume fresh herbs will yield better flavor.

Fix Use dried basil to avoid moisture which causes clumping and spoilage. Dried basil concentrates flavor and stores well.

Not letting basil salt rest before use

Why it happens Impatience to use the seasoning immediately.

Fix Allow the mixture to rest sealed for at least 24 hours to let flavors meld and intensify.

Variations

Lemon Basil Salt

Add 1 teaspoon of finely grated lemon zest to the basil salt mixture for a bright citrus twist. This variation pairs beautifully with seafood and salads, adding a fresh zing alongside the herbal notes.

Note: Add the lemon zest during mixing and store in airtight containers. Use within two weeks for best freshness.

Garlic Basil Salt

Mix in 1 teaspoon of garlic powder with the basil and salt for a savory boost. This blend enhances roasted vegetables and grilled meats with a subtle garlicky flavor.

Note: Combine garlic powder with dried basil before mixing with salt to ensure even distribution.

Spicy Basil Salt

Incorporate 1/2 teaspoon of crushed red pepper flakes for a spicy kick. Perfect for adding heat to pasta dishes or sprinkling over pizza.

Note: Add red pepper flakes with basil during crushing to infuse the salt evenly.

Herb Blend Salt

Combine dried basil with equal parts dried thyme and rosemary for a complex herb salt great for seasoning poultry and potatoes.

Note: Crush all herbs together gently before mixing with coarse salt for balanced flavor and texture.

Fine Grind Basil Salt

Use fine sea salt instead of coarse for a smoother texture that dissolves quickly, ideal for salad dressings and sauces where you want quick flavor release.

Note: Pulse the mixture briefly in a spice grinder to blend flavors without over-processing.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store basil salt at room temperature in a sealed airtight container for up to 6 months. Keep it in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight to preserve flavor and prevent clumping.

Refrigerator

Refrigeration is not necessary and may introduce moisture, which can cause clumping and degrade the dried basil. Keep basil salt dry and at room temperature instead.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended because moisture can condense upon thawing, leading to clumping and loss of texture. Best to store in airtight containers at room temperature.

Reheating

No reheating needed as basil salt is a dry seasoning. Use directly as a finishing salt or in cooking.

Make Ahead

Prepare the basil salt up to two weeks ahead to allow flavors to meld. Store airtight and use a dry spoon to keep it fresh.

FAQ

Can I use fresh basil instead of dried for basil salt?
Fresh basil contains high moisture, which will cause the salt to clump and spoil quickly. Dried basil is best as it’s moisture-free and preserves well when mixed with salt.
How long does homemade basil salt last?
Stored properly in an airtight container away from moisture and sunlight, basil salt can last up to 6 months while maintaining its flavor and texture.
Can I use regular table salt instead of coarse sea salt?
While you can, coarse sea salt is preferred for its texture and slower dissolution, which enhances flavor release. Table salt dissolves too quickly and can make the seasoning less flavorful.
How do I crush dried basil without a mortar and pestle?
You can place dried basil in a zip-top bag and gently crush with a rolling pin or the bottom of a heavy pan. The goal is to release aroma without pulverizing into powder.
Can I add other herbs or spices to basil salt?
Absolutely! Adding herbs like thyme, rosemary, or spices like garlic powder can create delicious custom blends. Just keep the herb-to-salt ratio balanced to avoid overpowering.
Is basil salt good for all types of dishes?
Basil salt works great on roasted vegetables, grilled meats, salads, and even popcorn. It adds a fresh herbal note and is very versatile in many savory dishes.
How much basil salt should I use when cooking?
Start with a small pinch since basil salt is potent. Taste and adjust seasoning gradually to avoid over-salting or overpowering the dish.
Can I grind the basil salt to a finer powder?
Yes, but be careful not to over-process it. A finer grind dissolves faster but may lose some of the basil’s aromatic texture and flavor complexity.
