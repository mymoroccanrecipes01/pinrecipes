This chocolate zucchini bread is my go-to when I want something moist and chocolatey but with a sneaky veggie twist. The zucchini keeps it super tender without overpowering the rich cocoa flavor. It’s perfect for breakfast or an afternoon treat!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this chocolate zucchini bread recipe years ago when I had a surplus of zucchini from my garden and a serious chocolate craving. I was skeptical about combining veggies and chocolate, but this recipe totally won me over. What makes this version stand out is the balance of rich cocoa and the subtle moisture from the grated zucchini, which keeps the bread tender without being dense or soggy. The addition of sour cream adds a slight tang and extra softness that you won’t find in typical zucchini breads. I’ve made this loaf countless times for family breakfasts and potlucks, and it always disappears fast. One trick I learned is to grate the zucchini finely and gently squeeze out excess moisture so the batter isn’t too wet, which helps the bread bake evenly with a perfect crumb. When it’s done, you get a loaf with a crackly crust, moist chocolatey interior, and little pockets of melty chocolate chips. It’s not only a great way to sneak in some veggies but also a delicious treat that feels like a cozy hug in bread form. If you’re new to zucchini bread or just want a chocolaty twist, this recipe is a keeper you’ll come back to again and again.

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, approximately 8 servings

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 cup grated zucchini (about 1 medium zucchini)
  • 1/2 cup sour cream
  • 1/2 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9x5 inch loaf pan. This ensures the bread won’t stick and will brown evenly. Proper preheating is crucial so the bread rises well and cooks through without drying out. If you skip this, you risk uneven cooking and a gummy texture inside.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven fully preheated, pan evenly greased with no dry spots
Step 2
In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, cocoa powder, baking soda, salt, and cinnamon. This dry mix forms the structure of the bread and the cocoa adds rich chocolate flavor. Sifting or thorough whisking breaks up lumps and ensures even distribution of leavening, which helps the bread rise uniformly.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients fully combined with no clumps, light and airy mixture
Step 3
In a large bowl, beat the sugar, vegetable oil, eggs, and vanilla extract until smooth and creamy. This wet mixture provides moisture and fat, which tenderizes the crumb. Beating well incorporates air to help the bread rise, but overbeating can make it dense.
⏱ Time: 3-4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth, pale, and slightly thickened
Step 4
Grate the zucchini finely and gently squeeze it in a clean kitchen towel to remove excess moisture. This step prevents the batter from becoming too watery, which would make the bread gummy. The zucchini adds moisture and subtle texture without overpowering the chocolate flavor.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini is finely grated and damp but not dripping wet
Step 5
Add the sour cream and grated zucchini to the wet ingredients and stir to combine. The sour cream adds a slight tang and extra tenderness to the bread. Mixing just until combined keeps the batter light and prevents overworking the gluten, which can toughen the bread.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet mixture is uniform with visible zucchini strands
Step 6
Gradually fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture using a spatula. Fold gently to avoid deflating the batter, which helps keep the bread light. Overmixing can develop gluten and make the bread tough, so stop as soon as the flour is incorporated.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is thick, smooth, and no visible streaks of flour remain
Step 7
Fold in the chocolate chips evenly throughout the batter. These add bursts of melted chocolate in every bite. Distribute them well so you get pockets of gooey chocolate without sinking to the bottom during baking.
⏱ Time: 1-2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chocolate chips are evenly dispersed throughout the batter
Step 8
Pour the batter into the prepared loaf pan and smooth the top with a spatula. This helps the bread bake evenly and rise uniformly. Avoid tapping the pan too hard, which can cause air bubbles and uneven texture.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter evenly spread with a smooth surface
Step 9
Bake in the preheated oven for 50-60 minutes or until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out with a few moist crumbs. Baking time varies by oven, so start checking at 50 minutes. Don’t overbake or the bread will dry out; underbaking leaves it gummy inside.
⏱ Time: 50-60 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted near center comes out mostly clean with moist crumbs
Step 10
Let the bread cool in the pan for 15 minutes before removing to a wire rack to cool completely. Cooling allows the crumb to set and prevents it from falling apart when sliced. Cutting too soon can cause a gummy texture and crumbly slices.
⏱ Time: 15-20 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread is warm but firm enough to slice cleanly

Pro Tips

Grate zucchini finely and squeeze out excess moisture before adding to batter.
Removing excess water from zucchini prevents the batter from becoming too wet, which can result in a gummy or dense bread. This step ensures the loaf bakes evenly and maintains a tender crumb without sogginess.
Use room temperature eggs and sour cream for better mixing and texture.
Room temperature ingredients blend more smoothly, creating a uniform batter that traps air evenly. This helps the bread rise better and have a lighter texture compared to cold ingredients, which can cause uneven mixing.
Don’t overmix the batter once flour is added; fold gently until just combined.
Overmixing develops gluten, which can toughen the bread and make it chewy. Folding gently preserves the tender crumb and ensures the bread stays moist and soft.
Check for doneness starting at 50 minutes with a toothpick inserted in the center.
Ovens vary in temperature, so checking early prevents overbaking, which dries out the bread. The toothpick test ensures the bread is cooked through but still moist inside.
Let the bread cool in the pan for 15 minutes before removing to a wire rack.
Cooling allows the crumb to set and prevents the bread from falling apart when sliced. Cutting too soon can cause a gummy texture and crumbly slices.
Use fresh cocoa powder for the best chocolate flavor and color.
Old or improperly stored cocoa powder can lose potency and develop off-flavors. Fresh cocoa ensures a rich chocolate taste and a dark, appetizing loaf color.
Add chocolate chips last and fold in gently to avoid sinking.
Folding chocolate chips in at the end distributes them evenly without breaking them up or causing them to sink to the bottom, ensuring every slice has melty chocolate pockets.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not removing excess moisture from zucchini

Why it happens Many cooks assume grated zucchini can be added directly, but its high water content can make the batter too wet.

Fix After grating, wrap zucchini in a clean towel and squeeze firmly to remove water. This prevents a gummy texture and helps the bread bake evenly.

Overmixing the batter after adding flour

Why it happens It’s easy to keep stirring to get a smooth batter, but this develops gluten, toughening the bread.

Fix Mix just until flour disappears. Use gentle folding motions to keep the crumb tender.

Skipping preheating the oven

Why it happens Rushing the process or multitasking can lead to putting the bread in a cold oven.

Fix Always preheat fully to ensure proper rise and even baking. Use an oven thermometer if needed.

Using cold ingredients

Why it happens Taking eggs or sour cream straight from the fridge can cause uneven mixing.

Fix Bring ingredients to room temperature before mixing for better texture and rise.

Overbaking the bread

Why it happens Waiting too long or baking at too high a temperature dries out the loaf.

Fix Start checking with a toothpick at 50 minutes. Remove as soon as it comes out mostly clean with moist crumbs.

Variations

Walnut Chocolate Zucchini Bread

Add 1/2 cup chopped walnuts to the batter for a crunchy texture and nutty flavor that pairs beautifully with chocolate and zucchini. Toast the nuts lightly before adding for extra depth.

Note: Folding in nuts adds weight, so be gentle mixing to avoid overworking the batter. Baking time remains the same.

Vegan Chocolate Zucchini Bread

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 tbsp water per egg) and use coconut oil instead of vegetable oil. Substitute sour cream with dairy-free yogurt for a moist, tender loaf.

Note: Baking time may increase slightly; check doneness carefully. The texture will be slightly different but still delicious.

Spiced Chocolate Zucchini Bread

Add 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg and 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves along with the cinnamon for a warm, cozy spice profile that enhances the chocolate flavor.

Note: Spices intensify during baking; add sparingly and adjust to taste.

Chocolate Zucchini Muffins

Divide the batter into a greased or lined muffin tin to make 12 muffins. Reduce baking time to 20-25 minutes for perfectly moist individual portions.

Note: Check muffins early to avoid overbaking; toothpick should come out with moist crumbs.

Gluten-Free Chocolate Zucchini Bread

Swap all-purpose flour with a gluten-free flour blend that includes xanthan gum. The bread will be slightly denser but still moist and chocolatey.

Note: Gluten-free batters often require careful folding and may need a slightly longer bake time.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the cooled bread wrapped tightly in plastic wrap or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days. Avoid leaving it uncovered as it will dry out quickly.

Refrigerator

Keep the bread in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The bread may firm up but will remain moist. Bring to room temperature or warm slightly before serving for best texture.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled bread tightly in plastic wrap and then foil or place in a freezer bag. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature. Reheat slices in the toaster oven or microwave for a fresh-baked feel.

Reheating

Warm slices in a toaster oven at 325°F for 5-7 minutes or microwave for 20-30 seconds. Adding a tiny pat of butter or a dollop of whipped cream enhances moistness and flavor. Avoid overheating which can dry out the bread.

Make Ahead

You can grate the zucchini and prepare the batter up to a day ahead. Store the batter covered in the fridge and bake fresh the next day. This lets the flavors meld and ensures a moist loaf.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for this recipe?
Yes, but thaw and squeeze out all excess moisture thoroughly before adding to the batter. Frozen zucchini tends to hold more water, so this step is crucial to avoid a soggy loaf.
What if I don't have sour cream? Can I substitute it?
You can substitute sour cream with plain Greek yogurt or buttermilk in equal amounts. These also provide acidity and moisture that help with the rise and tender crumb.
Can I make this bread nut-free?
Absolutely! This recipe has no nuts by default. Just avoid adding any nut-based mix-ins or toppings if allergies are a concern.
Why is my zucchini bread dense or heavy?
Common reasons include overmixing the batter, not using enough leavening, or adding too much moisture. Make sure to fold gently and squeeze excess water from zucchini.
Can I double the recipe to make two loaves?
Yes, but bake the loaves separately to ensure even cooking. Baking times remain similar, but check doneness carefully as ovens vary.
Is this recipe suitable for beginners?
Definitely! The steps are straightforward with simple ingredients. Just follow the folding and moisture removal tips for the best results.
Can I use whole wheat flour instead of all-purpose?
You can substitute half the flour with whole wheat for a nuttier flavor and more fiber. The bread may be slightly denser and darker.
How long does this bread keep fresh?
Stored properly at room temperature, it stays fresh for 2 days, and up to 5 days refrigerated. Freezing extends shelf life to 3 months.
