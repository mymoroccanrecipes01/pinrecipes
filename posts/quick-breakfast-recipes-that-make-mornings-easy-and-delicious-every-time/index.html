What You'll Need

Makes approximately 8 pancakes or 4 servings of frittata

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 tablespoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter
  • 1 cup shredded cheddar cheese
  • 1/2 cup diced bell peppers
  • 1/4 cup chopped green onions
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/4 cup milk
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Butter or oil for cooking

How To Do It

Step 1
In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt until well combined. This dry mix is the base for your pancakes and ensures even rising. Be careful not to overmix once you add the wet ingredients, as this can make the pancakes tough. Mixing just until combined keeps them tender and fluffy.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are evenly combined with no large clumps; texture is powdery and smooth.
Step 2
In a separate bowl, whisk together the milk, egg, and melted butter until smooth. Pour the wet ingredients into the dry and gently fold with a spatula until just combined. Some lumps are okay. Overmixing activates gluten and leads to dense pancakes, so stop as soon as the batter comes together.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Batter is thick but pourable with small lumps visible; no flour pockets remain.
Step 3
Heat a non-stick skillet or griddle over medium heat and lightly grease with butter or oil. Pour 1/4 cup batter for each pancake onto the skillet. Cook until bubbles form on the surface and edges look set, about 2-3 minutes. Flip carefully and cook another 2 minutes until golden and cooked through.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Pancakes are golden brown on both sides with a fluffy interior when cut.
Step 4
For the frittata, preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). In a bowl, whisk together the eggs, milk, salt, and pepper until smooth. Stir in diced bell peppers, green onions, and shredded cheddar cheese for flavor and texture.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Egg mixture is smooth and uniform with evenly distributed veggies and cheese.
Step 5
Heat an oven-safe skillet over medium heat and add a little butter or oil. Pour in the egg mixture and cook without stirring for 3-4 minutes until the edges begin to set. This step starts the cooking process and prevents the frittata from sticking.
⏱ Time: 4
✓ Doneness: Edges look firm and slightly golden while the center is still slightly jiggly.
Step 6
Transfer the skillet to the preheated oven and bake for 12-15 minutes until the frittata is fully set and lightly golden on top. Baking gently cooks the eggs through without drying them out.
⏱ Time: 15
✓ Doneness: Frittata is firm to the touch and a knife inserted in the center comes out clean.
Step 7
Remove the frittata from the oven and let it rest for 5 minutes before slicing. Resting allows the eggs to finish cooking gently and makes slicing easier without crumbling.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Frittata holds its shape and slices cleanly without falling apart.
Step 8
Serve pancakes warm with butter and maple syrup or your favorite toppings. For the frittata, garnish with fresh herbs or salsa for added brightness and flavor.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Pancakes and frittata look glossy and inviting with toppings melting or glistening.
Step 9
Clean up any spills promptly and store leftovers in airtight containers. Reheat gently to preserve texture and moisture for best results the next day.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Kitchen is tidy and leftovers are stored properly to maintain freshness.

Pro Tips

Let the pancake batter rest for 5 minutes before cooking.
Resting the batter allows the flour to fully hydrate and the baking powder to start reacting, which results in lighter, fluffier pancakes. Skipping this step may lead to a denser texture because the ingredients haven’t had time to meld.
Use a non-stick skillet and preheat it properly before adding batter.
A well-heated pan ensures pancakes cook evenly and develop a golden crust without sticking. If the skillet is too cool, pancakes absorb more oil and become greasy or fall apart when flipped.
Don’t press down on pancakes while cooking.
Pressing pancakes squeezes out air bubbles that keep them fluffy, resulting in a dense, flat texture. Let them cook undisturbed until bubbles form and edges set for the best rise.
For the frittata, use an oven-safe skillet to transfer from stovetop to oven easily.
Using an oven-safe pan prevents transferring the egg mixture, reducing mess and maintaining the frittata’s shape. It also allows for even cooking from both heat sources.
Add vegetables that are pre-cooked or finely diced to the frittata.
Raw chunky vegetables release moisture and may not cook fully, leading to a watery or uneven texture. Pre-cooking or finely chopping ensures even cooking and a pleasant bite.
Season the batter lightly and adjust toppings after cooking.
Over-seasoning the batter can overpower the delicate flavors. Adding salt, herbs, or syrup at serving lets you customize each portion to taste.
Use fresh baking powder and eggs for best results.
Expired baking powder loses its leavening power, making pancakes flat. Fresh eggs provide better texture and flavor, contributing to the overall success of the recipes.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overmixing the pancake batter

Why it happens Many cooks think mixing thoroughly is best, but overmixing develops gluten which toughens pancakes.

Fix Mix the batter just until the dry ingredients are moistened; some lumps are okay. Use a gentle folding motion to combine ingredients without overworking.

Cooking pancakes on too high heat

Why it happens Rushing pancakes on high heat causes burning outside and raw inside.

Fix Preheat skillet to medium heat and adjust as needed. Cook pancakes slowly until bubbles form and edges set before flipping.

Not letting the frittata rest before slicing

Why it happens Cutting immediately causes the frittata to crumble because it hasn’t fully set.

Fix Allow the frittata to rest 5 minutes after baking to firm up and make slicing clean and easy.

Using cold ingredients straight from the fridge

Why it happens Cold batter can cook unevenly and affect texture.

Fix Bring milk and eggs to room temperature before mixing to ensure even cooking and better rise.

Skipping butter or oil in the pan

Why it happens Pancakes stick and tear without enough fat in the pan.

Fix Lightly grease the skillet before each batch with butter or oil for easy flipping and golden crust.

Variations

Blueberry Pancakes

Add a cup of fresh or frozen blueberries to the pancake batter for a fruity twist. The berries add bursts of sweetness and moisture, making the pancakes extra flavorful and colorful.

Note: Fold blueberries gently into the batter just before cooking to avoid breaking them and discoloring the batter.

Spinach and Feta Frittata

Swap bell peppers for fresh spinach and add crumbled feta cheese for a Mediterranean flavor. This variation is perfect for a light, savory breakfast or brunch.

Note: Wilt spinach slightly before adding to avoid excess moisture in the frittata, which could make it soggy.

Banana Pancakes

Mash one ripe banana into the pancake batter for natural sweetness and extra moisture. These pancakes are soft and fragrant, a great way to use up ripe bananas.

Note: Reduce sugar slightly since bananas add sweetness; batter will be thicker, so adjust milk as needed.

Mushroom and Herb Frittata

Sauté mushrooms with garlic and fresh herbs before adding to the egg mixture. This adds earthiness and depth of flavor, perfect for a comforting breakfast.

Note: Cook mushrooms thoroughly to remove excess moisture before mixing into the eggs.

Gluten-Free Pancakes

Use a gluten-free all-purpose flour blend instead of regular flour to make these pancakes suitable for gluten sensitivities. The texture is slightly different but still tender and delicious.

Note: Ensure the baking powder is gluten-free and adjust liquid slightly as gluten-free flours absorb differently.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Quick breakfast items like pancakes and frittatas can be left at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely with foil or a clean towel to prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. Pancakes may become denser when cold; reheat gently in a skillet or microwave with a damp paper towel to restore moisture.

Freezer

Freeze pancakes or frittata slices in a single layer on a baking sheet, then transfer to freezer bags for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat in a skillet or oven to maintain texture.

Reheating

Reheat pancakes in a non-stick skillet over medium-low heat until warmed through to retain crisp edges. For frittata, reheat in a 350°F oven for 10-15 minutes wrapped in foil to keep moist. Avoid microwave reheating for best texture.

Make Ahead

Prepare pancake batter or frittata mixture a day ahead and keep refrigerated. For pancakes, cook fresh in the morning for best fluffiness. For frittata, bake fully and reheat before serving.

FAQ

Can I use almond milk instead of regular milk?
Yes, almond milk or other plant-based milks work fine in these recipes. They may slightly change the flavor and texture but won’t affect the rise or cooking process significantly.
How do I keep pancakes from sticking to the pan?
Make sure your pan is well preheated and lightly greased with butter or oil before adding batter. Using a non-stick skillet helps, and avoid flipping too early before bubbles form on the surface.
Can I make the frittata without cheese?
Absolutely! Cheese adds flavor and moisture, but you can omit it or substitute with dairy-free cheese. Just note the texture will be less creamy without it.
What’s the best way to store leftover pancakes?
Cool pancakes completely, then stack with parchment paper between each to prevent sticking. Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days or freeze for longer storage.
How do I know when the frittata is done baking?
The frittata is done when it’s set in the center and a knife inserted comes out clean. The top should be lightly golden but not burnt.
Can I add other vegetables to the frittata?
Yes, feel free to add cooked or finely chopped veggies like spinach, mushrooms, or zucchini. Just make sure to remove excess moisture to avoid a soggy frittata.
How do I make pancakes fluffier?
Use fresh baking powder, don’t overmix the batter, and let it rest for a few minutes before cooking. Cooking on medium heat also helps develop a good rise.
Can I prepare these recipes for meal prep?
Yes, both pancakes and frittatas can be made ahead and stored in the fridge or freezer. Reheat gently to preserve texture and moisture.
